## 3. Each item

Every item was reproduced on the real game engine with test scripts, then checked a second time by an independent review. Corrections from that review are included below.

### 3.1 Closing Day: $0 or $1

- **What happens.** Final day, Closing Day on (the default). The morning card says "Anything unsold tonight is worth $0. Sell it all!" and the shelf chip says "Unsold = $0". The Stocking panel says the jam maker pays $1. The engine pays $1 for each leftover ripe fruit. Tester's case reproduced exactly: 2 stalls, seed 2, two ripe leftovers, cash $95 → $97. Over 360 test games the payout always equalled $1 × ripe leftovers with Closing Day on, and $0 with it off.
- **Why.** The $0 lines only check "is this the last day", never "is Closing Day on". They were written before Closing Day existed (rules 4) and were never updated. The end-of-game recap counts the fruit the jam maker bought as "worth $0" and also mixes in green fruit, which the jam maker does not buy. RULES.md line 33, How to play and GAME-BRIEF say "worth nothing", which contradicts RULES.md line 122.
- **Two more bugs found.** (a) The Stocking note promises $1 even when Closing Day is switched off, and says "anything left" although green fruit gets nothing. (b) The final "Market closed" sales card is drawn before the jam payout, so its ledger leaves the money out. The only place the $2 is explained is one line on the results screen. That is why it felt like a surprise.
- **Fix (package A).** One shared sentence used by the morning card, shelf chip and Stocking note: "Ripe fruit left tonight goes to the jam maker for $1 each" when Closing Day is on; "$0" when it is off or only green fruit is left. Recap: "2 ripe fruit left at closing: the jam maker paid $2." Add the jam line to the final sales card. Fix the rulebook, How to play and brief wording. No rules change.
- **Computer players (package B).** On the final day the price helper and the computer players treat a leftover as worth $0 instead of $1, so they cut prices slightly more than they should (108 of 750 price decisions change at Normal level, 229 of 746 at Hard). This affects balance, so it needs a balance run. The jam maker cannot be farmed: fruit never costs less than $1, so buying fruit just to sell it to the jam maker breaks even at best.

### 3.2 "Enough buyers for everyone"

- **What happens.** You have 2 okay mangoes out. Bobby Books (Everyday) wants 1 and Captain Ahmad (Premium, fresh only) wants 5. Rivals have 3 fresh out. The panel says "Enough buyers for everyone (5 out for 6)". Only Bobby can buy your fruit, and you sell 1 of 2 at any price. The buyer chips on the same card already show Ahmad in red, so the card contradicts itself. A second case: okay mangoes priced $1 above an Everyday buyer's okay limit also get "Enough buyers" and sell 0.
- **Why.** The line adds up every order for the fruit and ignores freshness type and the price cap.
- **Fix (package A).** Walk through today's buyers in serving order against a copy of your shelf, the same way the engine sells, and say "Enough buyers" only if all your pieces could sell. Otherwise: "Only 1 of your 2 would sell at $7 (freshness or price)", or "Nobody takes yours at $8". The open-your-stall card's "N wanted today" gets the same treatment. The market-wide demand bar has the same blind spot but matters less; its legend can say "enough orders".

### 3.3 "Too old" when the price was too high

- **What happens.** Fresh apples at $5 for Inspector Wei, whose top is $4. The game says "Your apples were too old for Inspector Wei at $5." Fresh fruit could not sell at $5 either. Other wrong cases: okay apples at $1 for Premium Ravi ("too old", with a hint that okay fruit sells for $1 less, which is false for Premium); tired apples at $1 for Everyday Tan; a Premium Display stall told the wrong top.
- **Why.** The engine records one yes/no flag for "nothing qualified", and the explanation always turns it into "too old". The sale result itself is correct.
- **Knock-on bugs.** The same flag makes false end-of-day lines ("Rosa edged you out by $1 or less"; "You beat Rosa to a customer!" when Rosa's price was over the top), a false recap line ("Rosa undercut you by $1 on a $0 sale", "Rosa took 2 customers from you"), a false tie headline, rival taunts about a sale that was never contested, and "You offer 0" on the Showdown card.
- **Fix (package A), explanation only.** Name the real reason: over the top ("$5 is more than Inspector Wei pays for apples: $4 at most, even fresh"), fresh only (Premium), won't take tired (Everyday), or too old. This rule was tested on 107,133 random cases and always matched the true cause. Offers that did not qualify are left out of every "beat", "undercut" and "tie" line.

### 3.4 Juice Bar warnings

- **What happens.** A Juice Bar stall holds 3 tired blueberries. The sales card says "3 fruit on its last day spoils tonight", the day highlight says "3 of your fruit will spoil tonight", and the night animation shows them rotting. Next morning cash is $6 higher and nothing explains why: the morning card reports only spoiled fruit, and the $6 is already inside the next day's starting cash.
- **More of the same.** The forecast ignores Heatwave (3 okay berries: no warning, then "3 spoiled overnight") and Cold Room (warns on odd nights when nothing spoils). The "Juice Bar Regular" achievement ("earn $20 from your Juice Bar") only counts the +$1 sale tip, not the juice money: a stall paid $42 of juice did not get it.
- **Fix (package A).** One "tonight" forecast that follows the real night rules (Cold Room, Heatwave, Juice Bar): "2 spoil tonight, 3 become juice (+$6)". Morning line: "Your Juice Bar made $6 from 3 fruit overnight." Show juiced pieces as juice in the animation. Add an optional juice counter to each player's stats for the achievement and the results screen (old saves still load; it is never read by the rules).

### 3.5 Juice Bar salvage profit

- **What happens.** Confirmed. Buy 3 tired blueberries at $1 each, put nothing out, and the Juice Bar pays $6 that night: +$3, whatever the customers do. It can start on the night you build the Juice Bar (the $3 build pays for itself that night). Limits: 3 pieces a night; break-even on a Price Hike day ($2 tired fruit); nothing on Cold Room odd nights; no night after Closing Day. It costs 3 of your 6 daily buys and needs tired fruit in the market.
- **Size.** Most a player can make: about +$18 net in a 10-day game (+$12 in 8 days, +$30 in 14). Typical natural chance with Normal opponents: about $4 to $8 a game before the build cost. Normal and Hard computer players already use it a little (about $6 to $7 of juice a game); the trick itself adds about $1 to $2, which does not move win rates.
- **Context.** REP-086 found the Juice Bar and Cold Room nearly useless because computers rarely lose fruit to rot. The studio economy audit listed this loop as an untested idea (07-ECONOMY-BALANCE E8). No decision has accepted or rejected it.
- **Options.**
  - **C · Keep it and explain it (recommended).** It is a small, skill-based trick that gives a weak upgrade a job, and it is easy to follow on a table. Card tip: "Last-day fruit bought for $1 becomes $2 of juice." No rules change.
  - **A · Juice pays $1.** Saves waste, no profit on $1 fruit. Makes the Juice Bar even weaker. Rules version 10 and a balance run.
  - **B · Only fruit on your shelf before today's buying can become juice.** Keeps $2 but needs the engine and the table to track which fruit was bought today, which is fiddly on a table. Rules version 10 and a balance run.

### 3.6 Advice that ignores Rosa's tie win

- **What happens.** Rosa (Haggler) posts $7, you post $8, both fresh. The game says "Rosa undercut you by $1 and stole the sale! … At $6 you'd have sold 2." The number is right (a $7 tie loses to the Haggler), but the reason is missing, so it reads like "always undercut by $1". The same gap appears when Rosa played Favoured Trade.
- **More cases where the advice is wrong.** (a) Benny (not a Haggler) at $7 with okay fruit, you at $8 with fresh: the advice says $6, but matching $7 also sells 2 (fresher fruit wins the tie) and earns $14 instead of $12. (b) You are the Haggler: the advice says $6, but $7 wins. (c) Two buyers of the same fruit each get "At $5 you'd have sold 2" from a crate of 2. (d) You sold your only mango to a later buyer at $10, but the advice tells you to cut to $6 for an earlier one. (e) Both stalls played Favoured Trade: the game says "Rosa bought earlier today" when you did; Rosa actually won as the Haggler.
- **Fix (package A).** The after-sale advice follows the real tie order (Favoured Trade, Haggler, fresher fruit, earlier buyer) and the size of your crate: "At $6 you'd have sold 2. A tie at $7 still loses: Rosa is the Haggler, who wins ties." / "Matching $7 would have won the tie (your fruit was fresher): 2 sold." / "You still sold all 1 at your price." Presentation only.
- **Before the bell.** The pre-filled price comes from the computer planner, which treats a tie as a shared sale. In 360 test setups against Rosa it pre-filled her exact price in 262, and in 26 of them $1 less would have sold more and earned more. Package A adds a hint for people only ("Rosa wins ties"). Teaching the computer players the real tie order is package B (balance).

### 3.7 Scout shown as Gossip, and "unripe $1 now"

- **Scout.** After Scout, the walk-in card shows the ear badge "You heard about this walk-in (Gossip)". Both favours write the same mark on the card and leave no trace of which one it was, so the game cannot tell them apart. Only people see this: computer players never play Scout. **Fix (package A):** one neutral line, "You've seen this walk-in's card". Separate Scout and Gossip labels would need a new field in the engine and the online view (a small plan of its own).
- **"$1 now".** The set-up switch says "Some deliveries arrive unripe: $1 now, sell later. Mangoes take longest." That was true when the game began (every green fruit cost $1). Since rules 9, only mangoes arrive green (Mango Growers and Mango Season) and they cost $4 ($5 on a Price Hike day). **Fix (package A):** build the sentence from the game data: "Some lorries bring mangoes green: from $4, ready to sell after 2 nights."
- **Green watermelon.** The rulebook, the data and How to play still describe a green watermelon (1 night, $1), but no lorry delivers one any more. The market still draws its empty slot with a $1 price and the lorry marker on every screen, which probably made the $1 look real. **Fix (package A, as assumed in section 1):** retire it in RULES.md and How to play, and hide green cells on the market for fruit no lorry delivers green. Removing the slot from the engine would change saves and online play and needs its own plan; it is not proposed.
