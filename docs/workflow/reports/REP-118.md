How to read the tables: **Now** is copied from the code. In a template, `${...}` is a value filled in during play (for example `${cust}` is a customer's name). **[board]** means the words are drawn on a board the owner approved. **(check)** means the exact screen is to be checked when it is built. The inventory was made by a helper reading every screen's code; I checked the top changes and adjusted two (the stake line keeps "put down a stake", the rules' phrase; "Tied on cash, so fruit sold decided it").

## 1. Summary for the owner

I found about **1,800 text strings** a player could meet (about 150 are only read aloud to blind players). Most card
text was already rewritten in the plain voice and reads well. I propose **109 changes** (about 130 strings, as some
lines repeat): **12 errors** and 97 wording fixes. The **top 25** are on screens you see every day.

What makes the text sound unnatural:

1. **Rule-book voice.** "If you tie, you are asked after the prices flip." → "you'll be asked".
2. **No contractions.** "Mira will not take Soft mangoes." → "won't take".
3. **Two words for one thing.** "sold 2 at full price" (elsewhere "top price"); "Find an opponent", "match", "AI" where the game says player, game, computer.
4. **Clipped, code-like phrases.** "Nothing Mira would buy." → "Mira found nothing to buy."
5. **Names repeated.** "Rosa played Favoured Trade, so Rosa sells first." → "Rosa sells first: Favoured Trade."
6. **Odd joins.** Home shows "+$1 every customer pays" → "+$1 from every customer".
7. **Small slips.** "for 3 or 4, start a private game" (rooms take up to 6), "Champion!" (no exclamation marks), "See ONLINE.md." shown to players.
8. **"Piece"** is mostly used where the fruit is unknown, so I kept it.

Nothing here changes a rule, a number or what a card does.

## Names you may want to rename (owner's choice)

I did not change these, because each is a fixed name or a board word:

1. **"Auto load"** (Stocking button). "Load" sounds like a machine. Option: "Fill for me".
2. **"Open my stall", "3 out", "Stall open"** [board]. The glossary says to avoid "open your stall" and "out", but the Stocking board uses them. Options: "Crate ready" and "3 in crate".
3. **"Your takings"** (the takings sheet) [board]. "Takings" is British shop talk; Malaysian players would more likely say "sales". Option: "Day 3 · your sales".
4. **Trophy names mix two styles.** Some are in Title Case ("Open for Business", "Nothing Wasted", "Tie Breaker", "Comeback Kid", "Top Ten") and some in sentence case ("Every mode", "Ten mangoes", "Big sale", "Best day", "Hard win"). Pick one style.
5. **"Sole Supplier"** (trophy and title). The game says "serve a customer alone" everywhere else. Option: "Served Alone".
6. **"Stall level"** (Results, Wardrobe) and **"Level"** (Trophies) are the same thing. Pick one.
7. **Wardrobe items from an older look:** "Gazette Paper Hat", "Exchange Medal", "Banknote Bow Tie", "Certificate Sash". They don't fit a pasar. Options: "Newspaper Hat", "Market Medal".
8. **A few customers outside the Malaysian town feel:** "Cowboy Jack" (pans for gold), "Marcus Hook", "Otto Oddjob". The cast rules say not to rename for its own sake, so this is only a flag.
