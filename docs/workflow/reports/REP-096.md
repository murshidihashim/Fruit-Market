## 1. The rule

1. **Your stall is secret.** Nobody else sees your shelf, at any time.
2. **Buying is in the open.** Everyone can watch what a stall takes from the Wholesale Market, and remember it.
3. **After stocking, one number.** When every stall is ready, each reveals how many pieces are in its crate: not which fruit, not how fresh.
4. **At the bell, crates open.** Prices and crates are revealed together: which fruit each stall sells, how many, at what price. Shelves stay hidden.

No number on any card changed. RULES_VERSION 14 to 15; computer-player version 3 to 4.

## 3. How it is kept secret

- **One view.** The engine has a new function, `publicView`, that gives the game as one stall may see it: rival shelves blank; rival crates blank from stocking to the bell, with only a count. The screen is drawn from that view, offline and online.
- **Online.** The server sends each phone only its own view. A rival's shelf is never sent; a rival's crate is not sent before the bell. A test replays a whole game and checks every step.
- **Computer stalls.** They price through a new function, `believe`, which rebuilds each rival crate as a guess from public facts: the piece count, today's orders, and the shelf the rival was seen to buy (computer stalls remember purchases; they are made in the open). The real crates are never read. A test shows a computer stall and the price suggested to a person both come out identical whether a rival put out apples or mangoes.
- **Rival purchases** still fly from the market to the rival's box, because buying is in the open.

## 4. The printed game

Made outside the repo and sent to the owner as PDFs at rules version 15:

- **Six counter cards** (new, two to a page): one lies in front of each shutter. Empty until the bell; then the stall's crate comes out onto it, each piece in the box with the same letter and place as on the stall sheet, with the price tags face up.
- **Shutters** stay up all game. The stall sheet and the board's beat list say "say how many, not which fruit".
- **Guidebook:** stocking ends with "Say how many"; the bell ends with every crate coming out; unsold counter pieces go back behind the shutter; the Health Inspector is paid on your own count.
- Boards and pieces: 22 pages (was 19). Cards: unchanged, 145 cards. Guidebook: 15 pages.

## 6. Risks

- **It is harder to read the table.** Before the bell there is less on screen to decide from. People who do not watch rivals buy will price by feel.
- **Computer stalls remember every purchase.** That is fair (buying is public) but it is better memory than most people have. Easy stalls ignore rivals as before.
- **Computer stalls guess that rivals put out what is most wanted.** A person who learns this can fool them by doing the opposite.
- **Balance is untuned**, as in REP-094. The economy numbers moved by less than 1%.
- Unfinished rules 14 games cannot be resumed; the daily board starts fresh; older apps must update to play online.
- The repo's own print kit is still rules 13.
