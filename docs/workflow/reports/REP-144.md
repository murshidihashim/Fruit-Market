Each idea names the kind of mechanic and a well-known game that uses it, then says how it would sit on top of Fruit Market. Only the idea is borrowed, never another game's names, cards or look. As before, the day (buy, stock, price, rush) is left as it is, and nothing here is measured yet.

## 1. Things to do with a helper or a choice each day

### 1. One helper to send (worker placement: Agricola, Stone Age, Lords of Waterdeep)
- **What.** Each stall has one helper. In the morning, in buying order, each sends the helper to one spot in town; a spot takes one helper. Spots: the **Lorry** (buy first today), the **Kopitiam** (draw a favour card), the **Ice House** (one fruit of yours does not age tonight), the **Town Crier** (look at a walk-in), the **Bank** (take $2).
- **Early, middle, late.** As in those games, new spots open as the game goes on: the **Auction Room** on the first Market Day, the **Hotel** (a guest buys only from you tomorrow) on the second.
- **Why.** One small choice a day that blocks rivals. The poorest stall, who picks first, gets the best spot, which helps a stall catch up.

### 2. A job for the day (role selection: Puerto Rico, Citadels)
- **What.** Each morning every stall secretly picks one of five job cards, then all show: **Early Bird** (buy first), **Porter** (buy 1 more piece), **Haggler** (win tied prices), **Hawker** (sell leftovers for $2 each tonight), **Bookkeeper** (take $2). If two stalls pick the same job, neither gets it. A job nobody picked gains a $1 coin for whoever takes it next.
- **Why.** A second secret choice each day, guessing what rivals need. The coin rule makes the unloved job tempting.
- **Care.** It overlaps with shopkeeper perks; the perks might become the job cards.

### 3. Sleep in or rise early (the wake-up track: Viticulture)
- **What.** Each morning stalls choose a place on a wake-up track. First place buys first and gets nothing else; later places buy later but come with a small gift ($1, a peek, a favour card for the last place).
- **Why.** Buying order becomes a choice with a price.
- **Care.** It would replace poorest-buys-first, which keeps games close. The biggest change in this list.

## 2. Rewards for how you sell

### 4. Bonus for a big sale (bonus tokens: Jaipur)
- **What.** Sell 3 or more pieces of one fruit in a day and take a bonus token from that pile: the first token of the game is worth most ($4), then $3, $2, $1. Tokens are cash at the end.
- **Why.** A reason to go deep on one fruit, and a race to do it before a rival does. It suits a trading game and needs no new step.

### 5. First sale of the day (falling price tokens: Jaipur again)
- **What.** For each fruit, the first stall to sell a piece each day takes that fruit's "first sale" coin ($1).
- **Why.** A tiny reward for undercutting, so the price duel matters even when both stalls would have sold anyway.

### 6. Your regulars (engine building: Splendor, Wingspan)
- **What.** When you serve a customer's whole order alone, keep their card in front of your stall as a regular (instead of, or as well as, the favour card). Each regular gives a small lasting bonus by type: Bargain, your first Soft piece each day sells at no discount; Everyday, $1 off one piece when buying; Premium, +$1 on your first Fresh sale each day. Up to three regulars.
- **Why.** Your stall grows through the game, so the late game plays differently from the start, and each stall grows its own way.

### 7. A set of customers (set collection: Sushi Go, Ticket to Ride)
- **What.** Keep the card of each customer you served alone. At the end, a set of all three types (Premium, Everyday, Bargain) pays $5; three of one type pays $3.
- **Why.** A reason to chase a customer who is not today's best money.
- **Care.** It is end-of-game scoring, like the ribbons the owner did not take to.

## 3. Bluffing and betting

### 8. Declare your crate (bluff and inspect: Sheriff of Nottingham)
- **What.** After Stocking, each stall says aloud what is in its crate ("three apples"), true or not. One rival may challenge by paying $2. If the stall lied, it pays the challenger $4; if it told the truth, it keeps the $2.
- **Why.** The crate count is already public; this adds a claim to bluff with before prices are set.

### 9. Back a stall (side bets: Camel Up)
- **What.** Before the bell, each stall may secretly bet $2 on which stall (itself included) will take the most money today. Right: get $4 back. Wrong: lose the $2.
- **Why.** A reason to watch rivals' sales, a use for cash, and a way for a trailing stall to gain from reading the table well.

### 10. Haggle chips (adjust after the reveal: The Castles of Burgundy)
- **What.** A stall may hold up to 2 haggle chips ($2 each, bought in the evening). After prices flip, spend a chip to move one of your prices by $1 up or down. Stalls decide in buying order.
- **Why.** A second beat to the reveal: you see you are tied or $1 too dear and can fix it, if you paid in advance.
- **Care.** Favoured Trade does a similar job for ties and would need a look.

### 11. Push your luck at the seconds box (push your luck: Port Royal, Quacks of Quedlinburg)
- **What.** Beside the Wholesale Market stands a box of mixed fruit, face down. On your buying turn pay $1 to draw a piece; you may keep drawing. Draw a rotten one and you lose everything you drew this turn.
- **Why.** Cheap fruit with a gamble, and a moment of suspense in the buying step.

## 4. Other ways to run the evening sale (variations on D)

### 12. Everyone gets something (For Sale)
- **What.** Lay out as many favour cards as stalls. All bid in secret. The highest bidder chooses first and pays in full, and so on down; the lowest takes the last card and pays half.
- **Why.** Nobody leaves the evening empty-handed, and the fight is over which card.

### 13. The price walks down the row (sliding market row: Century, Through the Ages)
- **What.** Three favour cards lie in a row priced $1, $3 and $5. Buy one in the evening; the rest slide down and a new card arrives at $5.
- **Why.** No bidding step at all: wait for a card to get cheaper and risk a rival taking it.

### 14. Dutch auction (falling price)
- **What.** The lot starts at $8 and drops $1 at a time; the first stall to say "mine" pays that price.
- **Why.** Fast and tense in a room; one tap on a phone.

## 5. Trading

### 15. Swap at the market (bank trade: Catan)
- **What.** On your buying turn, hand back 2 ripe pieces from your shelf to take 1 Fresh piece of your choice, without paying.
- **Why.** A way out when you hold the wrong fruit, and a small plan around tomorrow's customers.

### 16. Sell to a rival (player-run auction: Modern Art)
- **What.** In the evening, a stall may put one lot from its shelf up for sale; rivals bid in secret and the stall takes the money.
- **Why.** Fruit you cannot sell tomorrow may be just what a rival needs.
- **Care.** Computer stalls need good judgment here, or it becomes a way to farm them.

## 6. My picks

| Pick | Why |
|---|---|
| **1. One helper to send** | The best-proven way to add a daily choice and blocking, and its new spots give an early, middle and late game |
| **4. Bonus for a big sale** | Almost free to add, and it changes what you buy |
| **6. Your regulars** | The stall visibly grows; uses the customer cards already drawn |
| **8. Declare your crate** or **9. Back a stall** | One light bluffing layer; pick whichever sounds more fun |
| **12. Everyone gets something** | The friendliest form of D for casual players |

As before: two or three at most, tried one at a time on the preview copy.
