## 2. How the rules work now [Code]

### 2a. Fruit: lorry price, customer price, decay

| Fruit | Farmers-market cost fresh / okay / not so / green | Customer top price (24 cards, after shift) | Price drop by age, oldest → freshest | Life on the shelf |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blueberry | 2 / 1 / 1 / – | 5–8 (shift 0) | −4 · −2 · 0 | 3 nights |
| Watermelon | 3 / 2 / 1 / 1 | 6–9 (0) | −3 · −1 · −1 · 0 · 0 | 5 nights (+1 green) |
| Apple | 3 / 2 / 1 / – | 6–10 (−1) | −2 · −1 · −1 · −1 · 0 · 0 · 0 | 7 nights |
| Mango | 4 / 2 / 1 / 3 | 7–11 (+2) | −3 · −1 · 0 · 0 | 4 nights (+2 green) |

- The farmers market cost is fixed per freshness stage; events change it ±1.
- The customer's price is the card value plus the fruit shift plus the event, minus the age drop.
- Fruit at the farmers market ages too, and every night all fruit moves one step.

### 2b. Supply and demand control (the tabletop problem)

- **Deliveries:** one farmer card a day lists three fruits (4–6 each); the fourth fruit comes as 2. Each number is then
  multiplied by a formula: 4 per shop × (0.8 + 0.2 × game progress) ÷ 17, rounded. Tomorrow's card is shown a day early.
- **Customers:** one expected customer, known a day early, plus 1 walk-in (2 from halfway), drawn face down. Every order
  size is multiplied by a formula: 4 per shop × (0.6 + 0.45 × progress), × 1.15 with 4 players, ÷ cards, rounded.
- **Selling:** every shop sets a secret price and quantity for every customer and fruit. Then:
  - cheapest sells first;
  - ties go to a Haggler facing a richer shop, then fresher fruit, then Favoured Trade, then turn order.

### 2c. What events, traits and upgrades do to outcomes

**Events** (from REP-025, income vs a normal day, 2p / 4p):
- Big gains: Festival +11 / +8, Superfood +11 / +5, Mango Craze +10 / +4 (the largest gap between shops).
- Neutral: Heatwave, Minimum Price.
- Losses: Slow Season **−12** / −7, Rainy Day −8 / −5, Lorry Breakdown −6 / −3.
- Most events hit every shop equally. They reward planning only when they are announced a day early, which they are.

**Traits** [Sim, rules 3]:
- 3 players: 30–37%, a 7-point spread (fair 33%).
- 4 players: 23–29%.

Close enough.

**Upgrades** [Sim]: building only one upgrade, against 10.8% for building none:

| Upgrade | Win % |
|---|---|
| Signboard | 20.7 |
| Cool Room | 15.8 |
| Handcart | 14.7 |
| Shop Bell | 13.5 |
| Juice Press | 12.8 |
| Bigger Shelf | 12.2 |

All are worth something; the Signboard is the clear best.

**Favour cards:** small one-off boosts. Fine on a table.

## 3. Proposed "Tabletop edition" (rules version 4)

### 3a. Core changes (tested)

| # | Change | Replaces | Physical form | What decision it improves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Printed deliveries.** Each farmer card shows 3-player amounts: its three fruits at 2–4 (today's number −2), the fourth fruit at 1. With 2 players, one less of each listed fruit (minimum 1); with 4 players, one more of every fruit | The supply formula | Numbers on the cards plus one line in the rules | None directly; makes the game runnable by hand with almost the same flow |
| T2 | **Printed orders and a walk-in rule.** Customer cards are used as printed. Walk-ins: players − 2 in the first half of the game, players − 1 in the second half | The demand formula and ×1.15 | The deck and one line in the rules | Same as T1 |
| T3 | **One posted price per fruit.** Each shop secretly sets one price per fruit on a 4-slider price board behind its screen, and puts the fruit it will sell in its "for sale" crate. On reveal, customers (waiting ones first) buy from the cheapest shop whose fruit is fresh enough for the price | ~10 secret prices per customer and fruit | A price board and a screen | The core duel becomes "what will they post for mangoes today?", which reads like a real market stall and is quick on a table |
| T4 | **Market pulse.** Each fruit has a marker on a track from −\$1 to +\$2. After sales: if any customer still wants that fruit, the marker goes up \$1 (shortage). If at least players + 1 ripe ones are left at the farmers market, it goes down \$1 (glut). Otherwise it drifts one step back to 0. Customers pay the marker on top of their card price | Fixed prices | One cube per fruit on a small track | Prices respond to supply and demand. Spotting tomorrow's shortage pays, and flooding a fruit is punished |
| F1 | **Fruit payouts lined up with risk.** Blueberry customers pay \$1 less; apple customers \$1 less again (apple shift −2) | Card shifts | Printed on the customer cards | See 3c |

### 3c. Fruit identities and payout [Sim, proposal]

| Fruit | Target playstyle | Cost (fresh) | Average sale | Net per fruit bought | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🥭 Mango | **Speculator:** buy green for \$3, wait 2 nights, sell big on Mango Craze or shortage days | \$3.61 | \$9.15 | **\$5.15** | Highest: 2-night wait, spoils fast once ripe, few delivered |
| 🫐 Blueberry | **Quick flipper:** cheap and plentiful; sell today or lose \$2 a night | \$1.72 | \$5.58 | **\$3.51** | High: 3-night life, steepest decay |
| 🍉 Watermelon | **Steady bulk seller:** big orders, slow decay, a 1-night green bet | \$2.51 | \$6.20 | **\$3.15** | Medium |
| 🍎 Apple | **The keeper:** lasts a week, sells any day, the safe stock for a Lorry Breakdown or a shortage | \$2.79 | \$6.08 | **\$3.00** | Lowest |

Now the more a fruit can hurt you, the more it pays: mango > blueberry > watermelon > apple. Under rules 3 apples paid
more than watermelons, and blueberries gave the best return on cost.

### 3d. Supporting proposals (not simulated: they need people, or computer players that plan ahead)

1. **The queue: forecasting the crowd.**
   - Tomorrow's walk-ins are drawn tonight and placed face down.
   - The card backs show which fruits each customer wants, but not how many or at what price.
   - With the face-up expected customer and tomorrow's lorry, players can forecast demand: "two walk-ins want apples
     tomorrow, and the lorry brings few".
   - Physically: printed card backs. Digitally: a row of silhouettes with fruit icons.
2. **Market Days.** Every third day is a busy fair day: +2 walk-ins, and the lorry doesn't come.
   - Shops must stock up the day before, which is where shelf space, decay and apples matter.
   - [Sim] Comebacks rise to 34% and blowouts fall to 31%.
   - But the computers don't plan ahead, and skill matters less in their games (Hard beats Normal 52%).
   - [Hyp] This may be the strongest inventory mechanic, so it should go to a paper playtest before any decision.
3. **Crates instead of sliding.** On a table, fruit goes into a crate stamped with the day it arrived. Its age (today
   minus that day) gives its freshness from a printed chart. Nothing needs sliding each night, and the digital game
   already works like this underneath.
4. **Events with decisions.**
   - Keep the events that are announced a day early and reward preparation: Lorry Breakdown, Mango Craze, Superfood,
     Heatwave, Bad Harvest, Hotel Order.
   - Replace the two pure shared losses (Slow Season, Rainy Day) with single-fruit shocks such as "Blueberry glut: +2
     blueberries per player, blueberry customers pay −\$1" or "Apple shortage: no apples delivered".
   - Scale Good Harvest by players: +1 of each fruit per 2 players, rounded up.
   - Never draw a night-only event (Inspector, Heatwave ageing) for the final day.
   - Needs a simulation round before numbers are fixed.
5. **Traits and upgrades:** no changes needed for the table; all are printed cards.
   - Juice Press stays the weakest upgrade (12.8%). [Hyp] It gains value with Market Days and stocking ahead.

### 3e. What it would mean for the app

- Rules version 4: old saves cleared; old apps refused online; separate daily boards (all already handled).
- **Pricing screen:** four fruit price sliders plus "how many to put out", instead of one card per customer. It is
  simpler and fits the full-screen sheet from PLAN-013.
- **Board:** a market pulse marker on each fruit column, and walk-in silhouettes in the street (if 3d-1 is approved).
- **Computer players:** must learn posted pricing properly before release. Today's stand-in is crude.
- **Rules page and how-to:** rewritten. The digital game and a paper version share one rulebook.

## 4. Risks

- **Posted prices remove per-customer pricing.** A shop can no longer charge a rich customer more than a poor one.
  [Hyp] Simpler and more market-like, but less fine control for experts. Hard beats Normal 62%, still under the 65% aim.
- **Market pulse adds one track and a rule after sales.** [Hyp] It is easy on a table, but it is a new idea for players.
- **The computers can't judge** Market Days, the queue or event redesigns, so those depend on playtests.
- **It is a large change:** a new pricing screen, computer pricing work, and a rules rewrite.

## 5. Playtest plan

1. **Paper prototype:** print the farmer and customer cards with the new numbers, use coins and tokens, play 3
   evenings with 3–4 people. Test T1–T4, the queue and Market Days. Time each day; target 6 minutes or less on a table.
2. **Digital preview:** after building, the same questions as REP-027 section 7, plus:
   - "Did the posted prices feel like a market?"
   - "Did you ever stock up ahead?"
