## 1. What "like D" means

D was liked; A (known events), B (three seasons), C (ribbons), E (repairs) and F (reserving) were not. D has four qualities, and every idea below keeps them:

1. **The day is untouched.** Buy, stock, price, rush stay exactly as they are.
2. **It is a new thing to do**, not a new thing to read.
3. **It gives cash a use** (REP-149: from Day 5 cash sits idle, $80 by Day 10).
4. **It is the game's own idea again**: a secret choice, then a reveal against rivals.

Ideas are grouped by where they sit in the day. None is measured yet; each would need a plan, a balance run and computer players taught to use it.

## 2. In the evening (next to D)

### 1. The auction grows through the game
- **What.** D's evening auction sells more than favour cards. The lot is drawn from a small deck built in three parts. Early: a peek at a walk-in, one piece of fruit, a favour card. Middle: favour cards, a third upgrade space, first pick of tomorrow's lorry. Late: a crate of 3 ripe pieces, or a guest (the Hotel Chef, the Wedding Caterer) who will buy **only from the winner** tomorrow.
- **Why.** One system gives an early, middle and late game: the prizes get bigger as the cash does. The guest lot is a real plan: win it, then stock for it.
- **Care.** The guest lot must not decide the game: its order is small and priced by the bidders.

### 2. One of each upgrade
- **What.** Each of the five upgrades exists once. On a Market Day evening the upgrades left are sold one at a time by secret bid (start price as printed).
- **Why.** Today an upgrade costs $3 to $6 and everyone can have any (REP-149: skipping them costs $4 a game). Scarce upgrades make stalls differ, and the bidding is a fight for the Juice Bar.
- **Care.** Two to a stall stays. A stall with none by the last Market Day gets a consolation (first pick at the printed price).

### 3. Phone orders
- **What.** Each evening every stall is dealt one order slip, face down to the others: "2 Fresh apples tomorrow, $7 each" or "3 blueberries in two days, $6 each". Take it or throw it away. A taken slip is filled from your **shelf** before the rush on its day; miss it and pay $3.
- **Why.** A private plan every day: what you hold back at Stocking now matters, and rivals see fewer pieces in your crate without knowing why. It is a small standing order for everyone, not one stall.
- **Care.** One slip held at a time, to keep it readable on a phone. Slips reuse the customer portraits.

### 4. The night hawker, every night
- **What.** After the rush, stalls may sell ripe pieces left in their crate to the night hawker. In today's buying order, the first piece offered fetches $3, the next $2, every one after $1.
- **Why.** A choice about leftovers every evening (dump or keep for tomorrow), and a reason to care about the buying order twice. It turns the Pasar Malam event into a standing feature; the event could then pay double.
- **Care.** It softens spoilage, which is already low (1.2 pieces a game). Best paired with an idea that puts more fruit on the table (5 or 6).

## 3. In the morning (more fruit to fight over)

### 5. The auction crate
- **What.** Beside the lorry each morning stands one extra crate card: "3 Fresh watermelons", "2 ripe mangoes". Stalls bid in secret before buying; the winner takes it on top of their 4 pieces.
- **Why.** It is where idle cash goes, and it puts more fruit into play, so more orders have two sellers and the price duel bites more often (REP-149: only 27% to 47% of orders are contested). A full shelf also brings real spoilage risk.
- **Care.** The winner must still have shelf space. On a table, 12 crate cards.

### 6. Tip the driver
- **What.** Before buying, any stall may pay the driver: $2 to buy one piece before the buying order starts. If several pay, the poorest goes first.
- **Why.** A tiny, clear use for cash on the days one fruit is short, without breaking poorest-buys-first.

## 4. At the bell (more mind-games on the secret price)

### 7. The chalkboard boast
- **What.** When crates are filled, a stall may chalk a boast for one fruit: "Cheapest apples today". After the flip: if it is true, +$1 for each piece of it sold; if a rival is cheaper, pay $2.
- **Why.** A public claim before a secret price. Rivals must decide whether you mean it; you can bluff a fruit you do not even stock much of. No new step: it is one tap on the Stocking screen.
- **Care.** One boast a day per stall.

### 8. Pay for a peek
- **What.** Before pricing, pay $2 to look inside one rival's crate (the fruit, not the price). The rival sees that someone looked, not who. A second peek the same day costs $4.
- **Why.** Information for cash, every day, and being peeked at is itself a signal.
- **Care.** Computer stalls must use it sparingly, or solo play feels spied on.

### 9. A voucher for tomorrow's regular
- **What.** In the evening, a stall may secretly pay $2 to hand tomorrow's regular (already face up) a voucher. In tomorrow's rush that customer treats the stall's price as $1 lower when choosing where to buy, and still pays the full price.
- **Why.** A one-day plan aimed at a customer everyone can see, and a hidden reason a "cheaper" rival loses the sale.
- **Care.** If two stalls hand out vouchers they cancel, and both have paid.

## 5. Across the whole game

### 10. A loan in the opening days
- **What.** On Days 1 to 3 a stall may borrow $5 from the bank at dawn. It pays back $7 on the next Market Day, or $9 later.
- **Why.** Days 1 to 3 are when cash is tight (REP-149: $2 left after buying on Day 1). A loan is a real gamble exactly then, and only then, so the opening plays differently from the rest.

### 11. Stall stars
- **What.** Each stall has a rating of 0 to 3 stars. Gain one for serving a customer's whole order alone; lose one when fruit spoils on your shelf. Stars win tied prices (before freshness), and at 3 stars the first customer each day tips $1.
- **Why.** Something a stall builds and can lose over many days, which makes the middle of the game about reputation, not only cash.
- **Care.** It overlaps with the Haggler and Tip Jar perks, which would need a look.

| 11. Stall stars | No | No | Yes | Medium |

**My picks to go with D:**

- **1 and 2**, because they make one rule for the whole game: *anything scarce is sold by secret bid*. D, 1 and 2 together are a single auction system with prizes that grow, which answers both of the owner's goals with one idea to learn.
- **7 (the boast)**, because it adds the most mind-game for the least rule, and costs no time.
- **3 (phone orders)** if a private daily plan is wanted; it is the strongest "planning" idea here but the biggest to build.

Too many at once would bury the day; two or three, tried one at a time on the preview copy, is the most the game should take.
