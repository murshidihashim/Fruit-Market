## 1. The direction

The review's main point holds: **make the price duel exciting and easy to read; don't add machinery.** The hook stays:

> **"How much can I charge before my rival, or the rot, gets me?"**

Every tweak below either:
- removes bookkeeping;
- makes the reveal clearer;
- gives a real alternative to "\$1 lower"; or
- gives the session an arc.

The new favour card is the **only** new information mechanism, as the review advised.

---

## 2. A new day: five beats instead of eight

| Beat | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Dawn: the "Tomorrow" strip** | The lorry unloads today's fruit. One strip shows everything known about tomorrow: the lorry card, the event, the expected customer (face up) and the walk-ins (face down, backs show their fruit) | All forecasting in one place, so there are no separate things to check |
| **2. Market** | Buy up to 4 fruits, poorest shop first | Unchanged |
| **3. Open stalls** *(new)* | Each shop puts the fruit it will sell today into its "for sale" crate, **face up**. Quantities are public before anyone prices | Makes the alternative to undercutting visible: "He only has two apples, so I can charge more and serve the rest" |
| **4. The bell** | Prices are set secretly on the price board, then **everyone reveals at once**. This is the centrepiece | One clear dramatic moment a day |
| **5. The rush** | Customers come in queue order; each fruit goes from the cheapest stall, and leftover demand **spills to the next stall**. Every sale is shown as customer → stall. Then night: fruit ages | Players see why they sold or didn't |

**Removed from the core:**
- the market pulse track, now an optional advanced rule;
- evening admin on most days (upgrades are bought only on Market Day evenings, see section 4);
- the Haggler tie rule. Ties become **fresher fruit, then poorer shop**, which uses one catch-up idea, not three.

## 3. Prototype A: the core market (readability)

| Tweak | Change | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A1 **Uniform freshness** | Every fruit sells at full price when fresh, −\$1 when okay, −\$2 when not so. Fruits differ only in **how many nights** they stay in each stage. One rule replaces four price-drop tables | [Sim] Plans win 26–38% (was 25–42%); Hard beats Normal 56% (same); comebacks 32% (was 28%) |
| A2 **Street prices (option)** | Each fruit has one printed street price (\$5 blueberry, \$7 watermelon, \$6 apple, \$9 mango). Customer cards show only what and how many, plus a "generous" +\$2 icon on a few cards | [Sim] Balanced (plans 25–43%), but skill matters a little less (Hard beats Normal 53%). Keep as the beginner option |
| A3 **Catch-up stays "poorest buys first"** | Keep cash order; don't switch to net worth | [Sim] Adversarial test: a shop that buys every upgrade wins 33% (fair), so there's no exploit. Ordering by net worth makes hoarding cash viable (13% → 32%) but makes upgrades optional, and it's harder to count by hand |
| A4 **Simpler ties** | Fresher fruit, then poorer shop | [Hyp] Fewer exceptions to remember |

**Prototype A without traits, favours and upgrades** [Sim]:
- Games are close (17% blowouts).
- **Ripening green fruit dominates** (60% wins), and Hard beats Normal only 52%.
- The layers we're removing currently keep that plan in check. If people find it the same, Prototype B must fix it.

## 4. Prototype B: different businesses (upgrades that change decisions)

There are four "stall specialities", one per plan. Each changes *what you do*, not just how much you earn.

| Speciality | Cost | Effect | Decision it changes | Replaces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold Room** | \$5 | Your fruit ages only every other night; Heatwave-proof | Hold stock for a shortage or Market Day | Cool Room (\$7) |
| **Juice Bar** | \$4 | +\$1 for each okay or not-so fruit sold; up to 3 spoiling fruits a night become \$2 each | Buy cheap old fruit at the farmers market on purpose | Juice Press |
| **Barrow** | \$6 | Buy 5 a day and hold 12 | Volume and turnover | Handcart + Bigger Shelf |
| **Premium Display** | \$8 | Your **fresh** fruit may be priced \$1 above a customer's normal limit | Stay expensive, and sell to whoever is left after the cheap stalls run out | Signboard |

- Removed: **Shop Bell** (the Market Gossip card takes over the information role).
- **When:** upgrades are drafted only on the evening after each Market Day (see section 5): three build moments a game,
  not ten.
- **Traits** stay as starting stall capabilities.
- [Hyp] The upgrades are designed to change decisions, so measure them by whether they change purchasing, holding and
  pricing, not only by win rate. The computer players need teaching before the lab can rate them.

## 5. Prototype C: market rhythm (the arc)

| Day (10-day game) | Rhythm |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | **Opening:** small crowds; learn the rivals |
| **3** | **Market Day:** +1 walk-in, **no lorry**; then the evening upgrade draft |
| 4–5 | Ordinary trading; demand grows |
| **6** | **Market Day**, then the draft |
| 7–8 | Second-half crowds (more walk-ins) |
| **9** | **Market Day**, then the last draft |
| **10** | **Last Call:** customers pay +\$1; afterwards the jam maker buys leftover fruit at \$1 each |

Market Days are printed on the day track, so everyone knows they're coming. In 8-day games they fall on days 3 and 6;
in 14-day games on 3, 6, 9 and 12.

- **Why:**
  - preparation, holding stock for a day with no lorry, becomes a planned, satisfying choice;
  - the jam maker softens the final-day dump without making stock valuable.
- [Sim] Market Days every third day:
  - plans become the most even of anything tested (24–35%);
  - comebacks rise to 38%;
  - spoilage halves.
- **But** Hard and Normal computers become even (50%), because the computers don't plan ahead.
- [Hyp] Following the review: lower spoilage is not automatically better. Test whether preparing creates hard choices,
  or just an obvious stock-up.
- A \$1 jam-maker salvage was tested earlier: no balance change.

## 6. The new favour card: **Market Gossip** 👂

> **Market Gossip** *(evening)*. Secretly look at the front of every face-down walk-in card in tomorrow's queue: who
> they are, what they want, how many, and their top price. Don't show anyone. Everyone sees that you played it.

- **Replaces Free Cash** (2 copies; the deck stays at 12). Free Cash is the only favour that is pure money with no
  decision.
- **Why it fits the review:**
  - it is the single, contained piece of private information that the review suggested;
  - rivals know you know *something*, and your buying gives clues ("why is she buying so many mangoes?");
  - that adds Stockpile-style reading without forecasts, secret objectives and trading all at once.
- **How it's earned:** like any favour, by serving a customer's whole order by yourself. It rewards the sales that
  build rivalry.
- **App:**
  - the walk-in silhouettes turn face up **only on your screen**, with a small 👂 badge on your seat for rivals;
  - online, the server must send the card fronts to that player only, the same privacy rule as secret prices, with a
    leak check added to the browser tests.
- **Needs:** the face-down queue (REP-031). It works whether or not the street-price option is used; with street prices
  it reveals quantities and "generous" icons.
- [Hyp] Test it on its own, after A–C, so its effect can be told apart (as the review advised).

**Favour deck after the change** (2 of each):
- Market Gossip *(new)*
- Free Fruit
- Good Riddance
- Fridge
- Favoured Trade
- Priority

## 7. App feedback: understand why you won or lost

| Feature | Status today | Tweak |
|---|---|---|
| Which customer bought from which stall | Showdown cards per line | One **"rush" view**: customers walk to the stalls they bought from |
| Why a sale was missed | Tags: "\$1 too high", "lost tie: fresher won", "Right price, not enough stock" | Keep; add "too old for that price" (freshness) |
| Day's money in, money out and waste | Day income only | **Day ledger:** sales, purchases, spoilage and upgrades, with net for the day |
| Reveal pacing | Fast, skippable | A short pause after the bell before the rush resolves |
| Forecast and rival stock | Lorry strip, rival drop-downs, rivals strip while pricing | All in the one Tomorrow strip, plus the public "for sale" crates |
| Playtest modes | Toggles for events, upgrades, unripe | Add **"Core market" (Prototype A)** and **"Market Days"** toggles in the set-up, so playtests can compare versions |

## 8. Test plan (the review's targets)

| Step | Version | Question | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A (core) on paper and in the app | Is the core fun on its own? | Teach in ~5 minutes; new players finish unaided; players can explain their best and worst decision |
| 2 | B (specialities) | Does specialising improve rivalry and replay? | Did upgrades change what people bought, held or priced? Did a "stay expensive" win happen? |
| 3 | C (rhythm), on the better of A and B | Does preparation improve the arc? | Hard choices before Market Days; the final two days still have real decisions |
| 4 | + Market Gossip | Does one private clue add reading? | Did anyone change their play after seeing a rival use Gossip? |
| Always | Same group repeatedly, plus fresh groups | Repetition and dominant plans; teaching problems | Replay requests; time per game; the reveals people remember |
