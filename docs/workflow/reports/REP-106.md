## 1. The game from start to finish, as I want it to play

The rules do not change. What changes is that every step is shown as a real thing moving on a table that stays put.

| Step | Today | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Opening and home | "Play full screen?" is asked at every opening. Home shows a picture of your shopkeeper that does nothing | Asked once. Home shows today's market, with your goals a swipe away (DEC-097). A paused game shows your table first |
| Starting | The Daily market and the Market League replace a paused game without asking | Always ask before a paused game is replaced |
| Morning | The top-up and the inspector's fine are a line of text | Coins travel to or from the stall, and the cash counts up or down |
| Market Day bid | The customer row empties; the panel says "Bid in" before you bid; nobody is shown winning | The row stays. Bids are turned over together. The winner's stake is placed on the order card |
| Lorry | No lorry is drawn. Fruit flies from a dot, and the box numbers do not change | The lorry drives in and unloads one crate for each fruit into its lane. The numbers count up as crates land |
| Buying | Numbers change before the fruit moves. Rivals take 9 to 12 seconds, one piece at a time, and a tap barely helps | The piece lifts from its box to your shelf; numbers change as it lifts and lands. A rival's turn is one move of about a second. One tap hurries everything until your next decision |
| Customers arrive | Swapped in one frame | Walk-in cards turn face up one after another |
| Stocking | The piece is in the crate before it gets there | It slides from shelf to crate; the crate is carried to the pricing table |
| Pricing and the bell | The market flashes back for about a second; the table blinks four times | One sheet stays on the table. Your tags turn face down, the bell rings, the crates open stall by stall, then a pause to read |
| Favoured Trade | The card is never seen being played, and the bell is shown twice | The question sits under the open crates. The card slides to the tied price and stamps it |
| The rush | The result is given away at the bell: tickets are already ticked and a won favour card is already on your shelf. One card for each fruit, 13 to 20 seconds | Nothing shows before its customer. One card for each customer (DEC-086). Fruit goes to the customer, coins come back, the ticket is ticked then. About 10 seconds. "Skip" always in reach |
| Evening | "Lights out" must be tapped even when nothing is left to decide | A built upgrade travels to your stall. The evening ends by itself when nothing is left |
| Night | The customer and rival rows vanish and the table jumps | The rows stay, dimmed. Every piece slides one box along; spoiled pieces tip off |
| Last day and results | The jam maker's money is never shown. The table is cut to the results | The jam maker's coins arrive. Totals count up, last place first. The winner is stamped. Then what you earned and what is next (section 2) |

**Time spent watching.** A typical day at four stalls holds about 39 seconds of watching today if you never tap. The target is about 21 seconds, and about 5 if you tap through. A 10-day game goes from about 6.5 minutes of watching to about 3.5.

**Five rules that keep it a board game.**
1. Things travel; they never just appear.
2. The table stays put: lanes, shelf, customers and rivals keep their places all day.
3. Nothing is shown before its moment.
4. Every move ends by itself and can be hurried with a tap.
5. Only position, size and fade are animated. No new library.

**Why it feels cut today.** When the Market Ticket look was added, its stylesheet switched off the older fades and slides. Most panels now swap in one frame.

**Three things I will assume unless you say otherwise.** The 21-second target. The rush moves on by itself about a second after each customer, and a tap moves on sooner. A rival's whole buying turn is shown as one move.

**Repairs that come first.** Read from the code and to be checked on a phone: "Skip" may be unreachable while a rush card is up; with reduced motion the open crates show for 0.15 seconds; the solo browser test does not know the Favoured Trade step and would not fail if it got stuck there.

## 2. A ladder to climb: the stamp book

**What is wrong today.**
- There are 43 achievements, 31 looks and 8 stall colours, but nothing adds up to a number that rises.
- Seven achievements cannot be earned, or almost never, under rules 18. Six looks sit behind them.
- The 14 stall looks and the 8 colours are drawn only inside the Wardrobe, although the screens say rivals see them.
- Online games do not count towards "games played".
- A signed-in player loses trophy progress for any name with a space in it (Uncle Benny, Ah Chen, some customers) at every sync. This is a defect to fix whatever is decided here.

**The proposal.**

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| One number | Your stall level, 1 to 30, shown as a stamp book. A full stamp card raises the level |
| Stamps | 1 for every 3 days played, 1 more for a win, 2 for the first Daily market each day, 10 for the weekly goal, and 2, 5 or 10 for an achievement's Bronze, Silver or Gold step |
| Six ranks | Roadside Table, Five-Foot Way, Covered Stall, Corner Lot, Shophouse, Towkay. The names are yours to change |
| Pace | About level 10 after two hours, 17 after ten hours, 30 after fifty. From computer games, not people |
| Achievements | 55 cards with Bronze, Silver and Gold steps. All 43 old ones are kept, so nobody loses anything |
| Looks with a place | Awnings, shopkeeper looks, signboard frames, titles, price-tag styles, card backs, table themes, bell and coin sounds. The picture and stub card backs you did not choose become rewards |
| The book | The 36 customer scenes and 21 event pictures fill a collection book: a silhouette until you meet the card |
| Hooks | A streak that forgives one missed day. One weekly goal. A "next reward" line on the results. No timers, loot boxes, lives or nagging messages |

The goals page on the home screen (DEC-097) would show the stamp card, the next reward and the weekly goal.

**First version.** The trophy fixes; stamps, levels and ranks; the achievement steps that can be read from a finished game; online games counted; the results lines; Trophies redrawn; rewards for levels 2 to 10 that need no new pictures; the looks that exist made visible on the table; the forgiving streak. It touches no rule, no save format and no online message.

**Money, later.** Nothing is sold now. The study's order: a one-time supporter pack first, then the printed game; no ads; nothing bought ever changes a rule, a price, the odds or what anyone sees. Three small things built in the first version keep that door open without a shop. The game brief still says "no purchases", so selling anything would be a separate decision, and a paid service needs its own approval. The store rules quoted in the study are from general knowledge and must be read again before any sale.

## 3. A single-player mode: the Market Month

**Why the two modes give nothing.** Both are an ordinary game with a label.
- The Daily market is a full 10-day game that can be replayed all day on the same shuffle. Its twists only take things away. Its one goal is a board that is empty until others play.
- The Market League is six ordinary games. It stores one number and gives one hat. A Normal computer in the player's seat wins the last stage 17 times in 100, so it ends in a wall.

**The proposal (working title: the Market Month).**
1. Pick your shopkeeper for the month.
2. A month is four market weeks. A week is a five-day game with one Market Day and a Closing Day.
3. Each week is on a street with a host shopkeeper and a house rule. Week 1 is Village Square. Before weeks 2 and 3 you choose between two streets. Week 4 is the Royal Market.
4. Rent is due every Friday. Your takings go into a savings tin and the rent comes out. When the tin cannot pay, the month is over.
5. Your stall keeps its upgrades and unplayed favour cards from week to week.
6. Finish ahead of the host and you win the host's card and a ribbon.
7. The tin at the end is your score: bronze for finishing, silver at $60, gold at $100.

| Street | Host | House rule | Rent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village Square | Kiki | Calm week: no events | $15 |
| Harbour Steps | Uncle Benny | Festival week: only the busy events | $40 or $46 |
| Old Town | Saleh | Fixed prices | $32 or $38 |
| Spice Lane | Pak Din | Hot spell: heat and shortages | $24 or $30 |
| Grand Bazaar | Rosa | Crowded street: four stalls | $32 or $38 |
| Royal Market | Ah Chen | The big one: four stalls, a Hard host | $42 |

**What it costs.** No new rule: every street is made from switches and cards that exist. The rules engine needs one small set-up addition (start a stall with upgrades and cards; build an event deck from chosen cards), with a test that a normal game is dealt and played exactly as before, so the rules version does not change. One new screen and small additions to four. Its own save slot, so it can never replace an ordinary paused game.

**Measured with computers.** A Normal computer in the player's seat pays all four rents in 90 months of 100; a Hard one in 99. Two tighter rent tables (79 and 66 in 100) are ready if people find it too gentle.

**The other two modes.** The Market League is folded into the Month: its street names, its place on the home screen and its trophy move across, and nobody loses what they have. The Daily market stays and becomes a five-day market where the first try counts, with a target score to beat. That matters because DEC-097 puts today's market at the top of the home screen.

**Risks.** Five-day markets and rent have never been played by people. The rent table must be made again after any rules change, including the three balance choices still open from REP-105.

## 4. The order of work to a live release

I recommend three releases, not one. Putting all of this in one release would hold back rules 18, the new cards and the smoother game for a long time, and would put an untested mode in front of players at the same moment as new rules.

| Release | What is in it | Gate before it goes live |
|---|---|---|
| A · the next live release | Rules 18 (built). Cards in design A with the new pictures. The screens rebuilt, each with its motion. The text rewrite. The trophy fixes and the first version of the stamp book. The home screen (DEC-097). The two defects (asking before a paused game is replaced; trophy names with spaces). The print kit | `npm run verify:full` PASS; you play the preview; your word to publish |
| B | The Market Month. The five-day Daily market | Final rules 18 numbers; the rent table re-made; a playtest by people |
| C | The weekly goal. Card backs and table themes as rewards. The deeper collection book | The usual checks |
| Later | Measure who comes back. A supporter pack only if the numbers show players returning | A separate decision and approval |

Release A in work packages, each checked with `npm run verify` and reported: (1) repairs and the two defects; (2) shared motion blocks and the cards; (3) the screens one beat at a time, the rush first; (4) the text; (5) trophies, stamp book, Trophies, Wardrobe, Results and Home; (6) the print kit and the release preparation.

I give no dates: I have no evidence for how long each package takes.
