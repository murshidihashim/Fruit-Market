## 1. Where we start

- The cards, player aid, stall sheets, counters and guidebook can be printed today (REP-124's print package).
- The market board, shutters, 200 fruit pieces, price tags, cash markers, chips and tokens came only from the earlier printing. The owner no longer has them, and there are no files for them anywhere.
- So without new parts the game cannot be played at a table.
- The freshness rule is the same in the app and at the table. Blueberries last 3 days, watermelons 4, mangoes 2 nights green and then 3 days, apples 6. The last two days are Okay, then Soft.
- Today the table tracks freshness by position: each night every piece on the board and behind every shutter slides one box to the left.

## 2. The freshness question: four ways compared

At a full table (6 stalls) there are about 50 to 80 pieces in play at night: up to 8 on each shelf (10 with a Handcart), plus what is left at the market.

| Way | How it works | Work each night | Parts | Mistakes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. A stack of cards for each piece** (the owner's idea) | Each piece is a stack of small cards, one per day of its life (3 to 6). Take the top card off each night. | Every stack in play loses a card: 50 to 80 actions | About 900 small cards (50 of each fruit × 3, 4, 5 and 6) | A stack missed or done twice behind a shutter cannot be seen. Stacks topple. Spent cards must be sorted back into stacks. |
| **B. A die for each piece** | Each piece is a die showing its days left. Turn each one down by 1. | 50 to 80 dice turned | 200 dice | As A, but with no sorting |
| **C. Slide the fruit** (today's rules; test pack 2 adds a crate tray) | The box a piece sits in says how fresh it is. Every box slides one box left each night. | 10 to 20 moves for each stall | Plain pieces | Same as A, behind the shutter |
| **D. Slide the ruler** (test pack 1, DEC-089) | A piece stays in the column it was bought into, numbered like the market's. A printed ruler beside the lanes slides one column a night. The letter on the ruler beside a piece is its freshness. | **7 slides at the whole table: one for each stall and one for the market, together, in the open** | Plain pieces, 7 card rulers | A piece put in the wrong column. Its number is printed in every box, so a buyer copies it. |

**Recommendation: D, slide the ruler.**
- It keeps the owner's aim, that freshness is easy to keep track of, without anyone handling pieces at night.
- Nothing ages in secret: every ruler moves in the open, at the same moment.
- The pieces themselves stay simple and cheap.
- Rule cards stay as they are:
  - Heatwave: slide two columns.
  - Monsoon: do not slide.
  - Shade Cloth: Pak Din moves his named fruit's pieces right first.
  - Fridge: move your ripe pieces one column right.
- The stall sheet and market sheet for D were drawn and checked in the tracker test pack (`print/tracker-test`). They have not been played at a table yet: that is the first check in the plan.

**Why not the card stacks:**
- About 900 small cards to cut.
- 50 to 80 actions every night.
- Rebuilding stacks whenever a piece arrives at Okay, is sold or spoils.

The idea behind them is right: a piece's freshness should be readable at a glance. The ruler gives that with one move per stall.
