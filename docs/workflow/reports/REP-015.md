## 1 · What this changes

## 2 · What makes *It Takes Two* feel polished (the qualities we can borrow)
*It Takes Two* (Hazelight, 2021) is a 3D console game, so we can't copy its look or technology, and
we must not reuse any of its art or characters. Its polish comes from qualities that translate to 2D:

| Quality | In *It Takes Two* | What it could mean for Fruit Market |
|---|---|---|
| **Toy-like, touchable materials** | The world is shrunk to toy size: wood, felt, clay, fabric | Stalls of painted wood, fruit like shiny clay or wooden toys, cloth awnings, chalkboard price tags |
| **Chunky, rounded shapes** | Soft silhouettes, big heads, readable at a glance | Rounder cards and buttons, bigger and simpler characters, fewer thin lines |
| **Warm light and depth** | Soft shadows, glow, layers of foreground and background | Gentle drop shadows, a warm late-afternoon harbour backdrop, depth between the table and the cards |
| **Everything reacts** | Squash and stretch, bounces, particles on every action | Fruit hops into your basket, coins bounce, buttons squish, the price reveal *flips* |
| **Expressive characters** | Faces and poses show feeling all the time | Customers smile, frown or shrug at the reveal; rivals react with a pose, not just a text line |
| **One thing to look at** | The camera always frames the important moment | Each phase zooms into what matters (market, customers, reveal) and dims the rest |

## 3 · Three art directions (choose one)

| | **A · Polished pixel** | **B · Toy-box tabletop (recommended)** | **C · 3D toy diorama** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | Keep pixel art; make it consistent and animated | A smooth 2D illustrated style: toy-like, rounded, warm, with soft shadows and textures | Real 3D: a miniature market you look down on |
| Fixes your (d) art style? | **Weakly**: same look, just cleaner | **Yes**: a clearly new, warmer, toy-like look | Yes, and closest to the reference |
| Fixes (c) feel | Partly | Yes: built around motion from the start | Yes |
| Phone speed | Excellent | Good (vector art; we keep the speed budget) | Risky on cheap phones; heavier download |
| Scope | Small | Medium-large: redraw fruits, ~30 characters, rivals, ~40 icons, UI | Very large: a new rendering engine and 3D models |
| Who makes the art | Claude, in code (as now) | Claude, in code as vector art (consistent, revisable), **or** a hired illustrator later | 3D modelling is beyond what Claude can do well; it needs an artist (paid) |
| Cost | RM0 | RM0 if Claude draws it; an illustrator would be a separate paid decision | Paid artist plus large engineering |
| Risk | Doesn't satisfy (d) | Art quality by Claude is good but not AAA; judge it on the style test before committing | Highest cost, slowest; could stall the game |

**Recommendation: B, proven by a small style test first.** It addresses all three of your concerns,
stays fast on phones, costs nothing if I draw it, and can be judged before any real work. If the test
looks too plain, the next step would be to brief a paid illustrator. That would be a separate, priced
decision under your RM500/month ceiling.

## 5 · Where PLAN-007 (the 9 broken-looking fixes) fits now
You didn't pick (b), and a new art style would redraw most of those screens anyway. Two options:
- **Keep it small (recommended):** do only the fixes that survive any art change. That's the grammar
  ("You offers"), the money pop going off-screen, and the sales text being cut off. Ship them in 1.1.2
  with Patches; park the rest until the new style.
- Or drop PLAN-007 entirely and fold everything into the new design.
