## 1. Diagnosis: what the strategic game is today

**The core loop (code).** Each day you buy up to 4 fruits (5 with a Handcart), then set sealed prices per customer.
The cheapest offer sells first, and freshness caps your price and breaks ties. The goal is the most cash at the end.

**What decides games (sim, current rules, 3 players, 600 games per plan on held-out seeds):**

1. **Two plans dominate.**
   - **Undercut**: always price \$1 under the expected rival. It wins 43% (fair share is 33%).
   - **Salvage**: dump old stock cheaply and keep buying fresh. It wins 51%.
   - In 4 players it is worse: 45% and 54% (fair share 25%).
   - **Premium pricing** (hold out for top price) wins only 13% in 3 players and 6% in 4 players.
   - In short, cutting the price is almost always right. That is the opposite of an interesting sealed-bid game.
2. **The market is loose early, so price wars pay.**
   - Customer demand starts at 40% of what the shops can buy, while farmers bring 100%.
   - Shops are therefore fighting over too few customers with too much fruit.
   - 11% of 3-player sales and 16% of 4-player sales happen at \$2 or less ("price-war share").
3. **The Handcart (and Big Basket) grows the market for everyone.** [Code] Demand and deliveries are sized from the sum of
   every shop's buy limit (`buyCapacity()` in `src/engine/game.ts`). So when one shop buys a Handcart, the town gets more
   customers and more fruit for all shops. That rewards whoever floods the market and blurs what the upgrade is for.
4. **Apples are a trap.**
   - Apples cost \$4 fresh but are priced \$1 below other fruit (`PRICE_SHIFT` in `data.ts`).
   - [Sim] Net profit per apple bought is \$1.64, the lowest of the four fruits (mango is \$2.58).
   - 25% of okay-age apples are still unsold at the end.
   - The "safe, long-keeping" identity does not pay, because almost nothing is held long enough for keeping to matter.
5. **Fruit is barely held, so decay rarely bites.**
   - [Sim] The average fruit sells 0.6 days after purchase, and 87–93% of fresh fruit sells the same or next day.
   - The only real "hold" decision is green mango (held 2.6 days, net \$3.33 each) and green watermelon.
   - Spoilage is 1.2 fruits per shop per game in 3 players.
6. **Early leaders snowball.**
   - [Sim] The mid-game leader wins 57% in 3 players (fair is 33%).
   - Only 15% of 3-player games and 8% of 4-player games are won by whoever was last at mid-game.
   - 45% of games are blowouts.
7. **4 players is squeezed.** [Sim] Average final cash is \$130 in 2 players, \$80 in 3 and \$56 in 4. Four-player shops
   barely grow, and price wars are most common there.

**Summary.** The tension the game wants is "freshness and timing against price". Today it collapses into "price lowest,
buy fresh, repeat". Fruit identities exist on paper, but only mango's shows up in play.

### 2c. Why decay is not the lever

[Sim] Shops sell almost everything within a day, so changing decay curves moves nothing. Decay only matters if holding
is a real choice. Holding becomes a real choice when the market is tight and has gaps: some days your fruit is scarce and
worth keeping, other days it is everywhere.

The package creates that through market size, not through faster rot. That honours "do not simply increase decay
everywhere or force a spoilage quota". Spoilage actually falls (1.2 → 0.3 per shop) because shops buy more selectively.

## 3. A deeper strategy layer

### 3a. The plans tested

Each plan is the Hard computer with one strategic lean layered on top (lab "styles").

| Plan | Lean |
|---|---|
| Undercut | Always price \$1 under the best rival guess |
| Salvage | Dump old stock cheaply, restock fresh |
| Premium | Keep prices near the top; accept unsold fruit |
| Reserve | Keep a cash cushion; buy only clear profits |
| Ripen | Prefer green mango and watermelon |
| Adaptive | Learn each rival's discount from past reveals |
| Turnover | Buy the cheapest stock and sell it fast |
| Event | Lean hard into event cards (festival, heatwave and so on) |

### 3b. Matchup table: one plan against two (or three) standard Hard computers

Held-out seeds 100001–100600 (600 games per cell, about ±4 points). Fair share is 33% in 3 players and 25% in 4 players.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Undercut | **43** | 25 | **45** | 21 |
| Salvage | **51** | 32 | **54** | 29 |
| Premium | 13 | 25 | 6 | 16 |
| Reserve | 30 | 36 | – | – |
| Ripen | 27 | 31 | 26 | 23 |
| Adaptive (reads rivals) | 36 | 34 | – | – |
| Turnover | 18 | 21 | – | – |
| Event | 32 | 30 | – | – |
| **Spread (best − worst)** | **38 points** | **15 points** | **48 points** | **13 points** |

**Four different plans at one table (4 players, fair share 25%):**

| | Turnover | Reserve | Ripen | Event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Now | 17 | 19 | 29 | 34 |
| Package | 16 | 33 | 32 | 20 |

**Reading this.** [Sim] Under the package no plan is a must-pick, and several different plans sit within a few points of
fair. Turnover stays weakest. [Hyp] That is acceptable, because it is the "no thinking" plan. Reading rivals (Adaptive)
stays strong, which keeps the sealed-price duel rewarding.

### 3c. Sealed-price analysis

- [Code] The cheapest offer sells first. Ties go to the fresher fruit, then Favoured Trade or Haggler, then buying order.
- [Sim] Price-war share (sales at \$2 or less) falls from 11.4% to 7.0% in 3 players and from 16.3% to 7.6% in 4 players.
- [Sim] Sales within \$1 of the customer's top price rise from 30% to 55%.
- **Meaning.** With fewer spare fruits around, the question changes from "how low must I go?" to "will they dare to go
  high, and is my fruit fresh enough to win a tie?". [Hyp] That is the more interesting guess.
- **Adaptive still wins 34%.** Reading rivals still beats autopilot.

### 3d. Handcart and Big Basket: the shared-market effect

- [Code] Today, any shop's Handcart or Big Basket raises everyone's demand and supply.
- **Proposed:** size the town from the base 4 fruits per shop, so upgrades help only their owner.
- [Sim] This one change does most of the work against undercutting (P1 test: Undercut 32 → 24, Reserve 32 → 39,
  comeback from last 23% → 28%).
- [Sim] Handcart is still bought in 99% of games, so it keeps its value.
- **Trade-off:** average cash falls about 12%. The demand start is raised (0.55 → 0.60) to give some back.

## 5. Test method and evidence

- **Engine copy with switches.**
  - The lab is a copy of the rules engine with switches for each proposed change.
  - With every switch off it reproduces production exactly (checked on 450 games, identical results).
  - The production code was not touched.
- **Paired seeds and seat rotation.** Every variant replays the same seeds. Seats rotate each game, so every plan plays
  every seat equally often.
- **Many opponent types.**
  - The plans in 3a, plus Normal and Easy computers.
  - Crude single-rule bots, which all lose under both rule sets (0–3% wins), so they are not reported.
- **Sweeps.**
  - Eight runs (a baseline, a plan check and six sweeps): 813 test cells and 384,200 simulated games.
  - Round 1: demand × supply grid.
  - Rounds 2–3: market candidates, apple and mango costs, delivery forecast, end-of-game salvage.
  - Round 4: traits, Handcart basis, broke credit, earlier walk-ins, 4-player demand, Slow Season, unripe cutoff.
  - Round 5: decay variants, trait sample of 2,000 games, 8 and 14 days.
  - Round 6: final candidates on held-out seeds.
- **Validation.**
  - Seeds 1–600 were used to develop the package.
  - Seeds 100001–100600 (and 100001–102000 for traits) were kept back for the final check.
  - The current-rules numbers on held-out seeds match the development numbers within the error margin (Undercut 46 vs 43,
    Salvage 54 vs 51), so the results are not seed-specific.
- **Market League check** (300 games per stage, a computer standing in for you):

  | Stand-in skill | Village | Harbour | Old Town | Spice Lane | Grand Bazaar | Royal Market | Daily win |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Normal, now | 65 | 67 | 54 | 38 | 11 | 5 | 8 |
  | Normal, package | 54 | 41 | 49 | 36 | 19 | 14 | 15 |
  | Hard, now | 87 | 84 | 84 | 80 | 54 | 28 | 41 |
  | Hard, package | 75 | 58 | 65 | 62 | 45 | 24 | 33 |

  The late-stage cliff softens, but early stages get harder because Easy and Normal computers do relatively better in a
  tight market. **Risk:** new players may find Harbour Steps harder. The league fix already suggested in REP-025 should
  follow this package, and the playtest checks it.
- **Rejected or no measurable effect:**
  - credit for broke shops;
  - earlier second walk-in;
  - Slow Season at −\$1;
  - Bargain Hunter minimum cost;
  - Big Basket shelf bonus;
  - unripe cutoff;
  - end-of-game salvage or juice;
  - apple and blueberry decay changes.
- **Optional, not in the package:**
  - **Showing tomorrow's farmer delivery.** Undercut 28%, Turnover 14%, Hard-vs-Normal gap up to 72% in 2 players. It is
    an information feature with UI cost.
  - **Green mango at \$3.**
- **Coverage gaps (NOT RUN):**
  - human play;
  - online rooms under new rules (needs verify:full after the patch);
  - Juice Press and Cool Room rework (Juice Press stays near-useless: 0.24 juiced per game);
  - favour-card balance;
  - every event card individually;
  - 2-player plan matchups (only mirror and skill tests);
  - Easy-level opponents in the plan tests.
- **Reproducibility.**
  - The lab, experiment lists and raw results are archived as `docs/workflow/reports/img/REP-027-lab.tar.gz`, with a README
    inside.

### 6a. Essential changes

For each change: what decision it improves, why it improves that decision, and what it could break.

| # | Change | Decision improved | Why | What could break |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Tighter town.** Demand starts at 60% of base capacity instead of 40% (growth unchanged at +45%). ## 7. Human playtest plan

- **Who.** 4–6 people. Mix newcomers and people who have played 3+ games. At least one session with 4 players on phones
  (pass-and-play or online).
- **Format.**
  - Each tester plays current rules (live game) and the package (a preview copy built from a branch), in alternating order.
  - Each set is 2 games: one Standard and one Quick or Long.
  - Testers are not told which version is which.
- **Questions after each game** (1–5 scale, plus one sentence):
  1. Did you have more than one sensible way to play?
  2. Did prices feel like a guessing duel or a race to the bottom?
  3. Did each fruit feel different? Which one did you avoid, and why?
  4. Did you feel you could still win in the last third?
  5. Was the game the right length?
  6. Was anything confusing?
- **Watch for:**
  - the price testers set relative to the top price;
  - whether anyone buys a Handcart and says why;
  - whether apples get bought;
  - league stage 2 difficulty for newcomers.
- **Pass mark.**
  - The package scores at least as well on questions 1, 2 and 4 for most testers.
  - No tester finds it more confusing.
  - Any FAIL goes back to the lab.
- **No telemetry.** Notes are written by hand. No new data collection, as instructed.
