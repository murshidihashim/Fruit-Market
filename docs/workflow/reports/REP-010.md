## 2 · Does the theme show up in what players do?

| Repeated action | How the theme expresses it | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Buying at the farmers market; poorest buys first | A morning market where small shops get first pick | **Strong**. The catch-up rule feels like market goodwill |
| Fruit ages each night; older fruit sells for less; spoiled fruit is binned | The freshness track and "Not so" fruit | **Strong**. This *is* the fantasy; every choice is a race against rot |
| Secret prices, all revealed at once; cheapest sells first | "Showdown" reveal, customer by customer | **Mixed**. Exciting and fair, but real stalls post prices openly. The reveal needs a stall-side reason (presentation idea in section 5) |
| Customers with orders and price caps | 24 named characters with quirks | **Strong** for orders and caps. The quirks are cosmetic (they never change play), which is fine but should stay labelled cosmetic |
| Events (Heatwave, Health Inspector, Lorry Breakdown…) | Each bends the market in a believable way | **Strong**. Heatwave aging fruit and the Inspector fining old fruit are the best theme-mechanic matches |
| Upgrades (Handcart, Cool Room, Juice Press, Signboard…) | Things a stall owner would actually buy | **Strong** |
| Favour cards | Rewards for serving a whole order yourself | **Mixed**. "Free Cash" and "Good Riddance" read as abstract game cards rather than market favours |
| Rival shops (animal traders) | Six characters with reaction lines | **Mixed**. Fun, but personality and play style don't match: lines are cosmetic, and how cleverly they price comes from difficulty, not character |

Overall the core loop expresses the fantasy well. The weak spots are presentation: why prices are
secret, a few abstract card names, and cast identity. None needs a rule change.

## 3 · Proposed design pillars (for your approval: DEC-015)

| Pillar | Include when… | Exclude when… |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Fresh fruit, fast money:** every decision is a race against spoilage | It makes freshness visible or raises the cost of waiting | It lets fruit sit forever at no cost, or hides freshness |
| **2. Outprice rivals in secret:** commit a price blind, then see the reveal | It keeps prices hidden until the reveal and makes the reveal explain *why* | It shows rivals' prices early, or adds open haggling or chat |
| **3. A cheerful town market:** playful characters, short sessions, readable on a phone | Jokes target behaviour (a vampire who hates garlic); games stay under about 20 min | Mean or stereotype-based jokes, grind, pay-to-win, long text |

## 4 · Coherence issues found (review before expanding)

1. **Mixed setting identity** *(owner decision, see question 1)*:
   - Customer names are Malaysian and multicultural (Aisyah, Mei Ling, Kumar, Wei Jie), and one rival is "Uncle Toad".
   - The palette is Mediterranean (terracotta, harbour teal, olive).
   - Prices are in **$**, and the fruits are blueberry, watermelon, apple and mango (two are imports in Malaysia).

   It reads as "anywhere town". That's fine, but it isn't a choice anyone made deliberately.
2. **Possible ethnic association:** "Panda Lim" pairs a Chinese surname with a panda. Most other names pair
   freely with professions or fantasy roles (Astronaut Aisyah, Ninja Farid), so this one stands out.
   Recommend renaming it (cosmetic only). "Kumar the Genie" is milder; worth a second look.
3. **Why are prices secret?** The best moment in the game has no in-world reason. That's a presentation
   fix, not a rule change (section 5).
4. **Repeated jokes:** each rival has 2–3 lines per situation, and a 10-day game has many reveals, so lines
   probably repeat. Hypothesis; check in the playtest.
5. **Icon style:** some emoji still sit beside the pixel art (🔒 Prices locked, 📊, 🙋/🤖, 🕵️). Already in
   backlog B-33.

## 5 · Representative day (actual rules)

*Day 4 of 10, Standard game. You and Foxy Rosa (computer). You have $14; she has $19.*

1. **Morning:** the event is 🔥 **Heatwave**: watermelons sell for $4 more, but all fruit ages an extra step
   tonight. Tomorrow's event, shown early: 🕵️ Health Inspector.
2. **Buy:** you're poorer, so you **buy first**. Fresh watermelon costs $3. You take 3 and leave the cheap
   blueberries: in a heatwave they'd rot by tomorrow. *Choice: profit now vs. stock that survives tonight.*
3. **Customers:** Cowboy John wants 2 watermelons and pays up to $6 + $4 = **$10** for fresh ones.
4. **Secret price:** you offer 2 at **$9**; Rosa secretly offers 2 at $8.
5. **Showdown:** prices flip; the cheapest sells first. Rosa's $8 fills John's order, and you sell nothing.
   Rosa quips "A dollar cheaper. Sorry, not sorry!" *Feedback:* your offer is tagged "$1 too high".
6. **Evening:** you build the ❄️ **Cool Room** ($7). Your fruit now ages only every other night, and heatwaves
   can't touch it. *Choice: spend on protection or keep cash for tomorrow.*
7. **Night:** heatwave aging hits Rosa's shelf. Your Cool Room saves your 3 watermelons for tomorrow's customers,
   and tomorrow's Inspector will fine Rosa for her old fruit.

**Theme-only idea for pillar 2 (not approved, no rule change):** present secret pricing as *chalkboards*.
Each stall chalks its price face-down, the market bell rings, and the boards flip. Same rule, with a
market reason.

## 6 · Mini creative bible (draft: samples only, per the companion)

- **World and tone:** a sunny town market where anyone might shop: cowboys, astronauts, a vampire after
  sunset. Light, cheeky, never mean. Stakes: a good day's takings, never ruin.
- **Sample character:** *Grandpa Tan* (cosmetic). Silhouette: cardigan, cane, paper bag. Desire: a fair
  price. Flaw: remembers when apples cost ten cents. Sample reaction when you sell at full price: "Ten
  dollars? In my day…"
- **Sample event:** *Health Inspector*. The clipboard arrives at night; every Not-so fruit costs $2.
  This teaches "sell old fruit first" without a tutorial line.
- **Key screen:** the reveal. Face-down boards flip highest to lowest, the winner's coins fly to their
  stall, and the losing offer is tagged "$1 too high". It works without sound and without colour alone:
  text plus movement.
- **Do:** jokes about what characters *do*; short lines; a mix of names and roles across cultures.
  **Avoid:** pairing a person's ethnicity with an animal or cultural cliché; jokes longer than one line; new lore that doesn't change a decision.

## 7 · Riskiest creative hypothesis and the smallest test (DEC-016)

**Hypothesis:** first-time players understand *why* they won or lost a sale at the reveal. That is pillar 2,
and it's what makes the game feel fair rather than random.

**Format:** a playtest with the existing game (preview or live 1.1.1). **No code needed.** It can't tell us
retention, market demand or online-server behaviour.

**Playtest card**

| | |
|---|---|
| Testers | 3–5 people who have never played; no coaching |
| Setup | Their own phone. New game → Quick (8 days) → 1 computer shop (Normal) |
| Tasks | 1) Start a game. 2) Buy fruit. 3) Set your prices. 4) After the first sales, say why you sold or didn't. 5) Finish the game |
| Watch for | Time to first price; whether they mention "cheapest sells first" or freshness; whether they read the event card; where they hesitate |
| Ask after | "What were you trying to do?" "Why did you lose that customer?" "Would you play again?" (note if they start another game unprompted) |
| Provisional criteria (you set them; these are suggestions, not benchmarks) | **Continue** if at least 3 of 4 can explain a lost sale by day 3. **Revise** (reveal presentation, section 5) if 2 or fewer can |
| Limits | Small sample; friends are kind; not proof of fun or retention |

I'll record what happens (observations separate from interpretations) in the next report.
