# REP-113 · Do standing orders pay, with the stake and the cost of the fruit?

| | |
|---|---|
| Game | Fruit Market 1.20.0 live, rules version 18 (the working branch has the same rules) |
| Report date | 2026-11-12 (Asia/Kuala_Lumpur) |
| Type | Analysis for a decision |
| Request | Owner, 2026-11-12: "analyse if standing order makes sense with the cost involved" |
| Approval | Nothing is changed by this report. Options in section 5 are for the owner to choose. |

## 1. Short answer

Yes for a careful bidder, but only just. Winning a standing order at a sensible bid roughly doubles what each piece earns, yet over a whole game it adds only about $1 to $1.60 to a stall's cash (about 1 to 2 per cent), because the order uses the same day's buying, shelf and cash that would otherwise sell in the rush. Reckless bids lose a lot: the stake does its job. Mango orders are the weak spot: about half find no bidder, and half of those taken fail.

## 2. What one order costs and pays (the card numbers)

| Order | Highest bid | Stake | Pieces at 2–3 / 4 / 5–6 stalls | Cost to buy a piece | Profit a piece at the highest bid | The same piece sold in the rush (average sale price less cost, 4 stalls) | Cash needed the day you win, 4 stalls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blueberry | $9 | $6 | 3 / 4 / 5 | $2 Fresh, $1 Okay | $7 | $2.42 | $6, the pieces later (they last 3 days, so buy them on the last two days) |
| Watermelon | $12 | $8 | 3 / 4 / 5 | $3 | $9 | $2.81 | $8, the pieces over three buying turns |
| Apple | $11 | $7 | 3 / 4 / 5 | $3 | $8 | $2.36 | $7, the pieces over three buying turns |
| Mango | $18 | $12 | 3 / 4 / 5 | $4 Green | $14 | $6.51 | $28: the stake and four Green mangoes that same day, so they ripen in time |

An Order Book halves the stake and pays $2 more a piece.

## 3. What happens in play (computer games, rules 18 as built)

1,500 games each at 3, 4 and 6 stalls with Normal computers, and 1,000 at 4 stalls with Hard computers, 10 days.

| 4 stalls, Normal | Blueberry | Watermelon | Apple | Mango |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nobody bid | 5% | 2% | 4% | 56% |
| Bidders an order | 3.2 | 2.9 | 2.9 | 0.9 |
| Winning bid (share of the highest bid) | $6.29 (70%) | $7.82 (65%) | $7.44 (68%) | $13.29 (74%) |
| Paid a piece, with the Order Book's $2 | $8.05 | $9.34 | $8.92 | $14.86 |
| Delivered in full | 76% | 83% | 80% | 51% |
| Stake lost for each order held | $1.31 | $1.29 | $1.23 | $5.01 |
| The holder's margin on the order (paid less cost, less the stake lost) | $20.38 | $22.19 | $20.21 | $27.18 |

At 3 stalls mango orders find no bidder 44% of the time; at 6 stalls 49%, and only 39% of those taken are delivered in full. Hard computers bid lower (57 to 68% of the highest bid) and deliver a little more often.

The stall that holds an order ends its three days $7.53 (3 stalls), $11.71 (4) and $15.86 (6) ahead of the average rival. That flatters orders: the holder is the stall that was best placed to bid lowest.

## 4. The fair test: the same seat, made to bid one way (600 paired games a row)

| Seat's rule | Cash against bidding as the computer does, 3 stalls | 4 stalls |
|---|---|---|
| Bids as the computer does (weighs whether it can deliver) | 0 | 0 |
| Always passes | −$1.57 (SE 0.64) | −$0.88 (SE 0.58) |
| Always bids the highest bid | −$0.93 (SE 0.74) | −$0.80 (SE 0.73) |
| Always bids 60% of the highest bid | −$6.58 | −$3.84 |
| Always bids half | −$10.20 | −$7.95 |
| Always bids $1 | −$28.92 | −$31.78 |

So: a careful bid is worth about $1 to $1.60 a game over never bidding; bidding the highest bid wins too seldom to matter; low bids lose stakes (about $4 to $7 a game) and short deliveries.

## 5. Why the margin is large but the gain is small

- **The order replaces rush sales, it does not add to them.** A stall buys at most 4 pieces a day (5 with a Handcart) and holds 8. Pieces bought for the order are pieces not bought for the row.
- **The cash is tied up on the morning it is won**, when a stall usually has $15 to $25: the stake comes out before buying.
- **Every stall wants the same lorry.** When the lorry brings few of that fruit, the holder can come up short and lose the stake.
- **Mango** needs the stake and the Green mangoes on the same day ($24 to $32), which most stalls do not have on Day 3, and Green mangoes are scarce.

## 6. Options for the owner

1. **Leave it as it is.** It is a small plus for planning ahead and the stake stops wild bids. Mango orders stay mostly unbid (DEC-101 kept them for now).
2. **Show the sums on the bid sheet (no rule change).** For example: "4 watermelons cost about $12 at the market today. At $10 a piece you clear about $28 if you deliver all 4." This helps a person bid well without changing the game. Recommended in any case.
3. **Make mango orders workable (a rules change, rules version 19).** For example: a mango order is due in 3 days instead of 2, so the mangoes can be bought the day after winning; or the mango stake is $8. Needs a balance run first.
4. **Make orders worth more (a rules change).** For example: the order's holder buys first on the due day, so it is less often short; or a full delivery pays a $3 bonus. Needs a balance run first.
