## 1. The short version

1. **Recommendation: a controlled hybrid, "Look E".** The world, frames and graphics take the crate-label style of "The Great Fruit Exchange". The people take the personality of "Orchard & Oddballs", but stay ordinary townsfolk. Everything is drawn in one flat screen-print style in today's palette, plus a few additions for your approval.
2. **Three AI review passes agreed.** Each was written from one viewpoint (tabletop publisher, art-production lead, game identity) and scored the options out of 45: hybrid 35 to 36, Oddballs alone 25 to 27, Exchange alone 22 to 25. They are not outside experts. Treat them as a structured opinion. The scores per criterion are in appendix A.
3. **The pixel look you like stays on the phone's board.** In the rule "the board shows tokens; cards show portraits", illustrated portraits go on printed cards, the box, the store and a few card views in the app. **The trade-off:** during play the phone shows the same people as pixel tokens, not as the new pictures (question 2).
4. **Pilot characters: Benny (shopkeeper) and Sailor Jia Wen (customer).** They are the most different pair that is safe for hidden information.
5. **Biggest commercial risk: AI art on Kickstarter.** From search summaries, not yet checked on the source pages: Kickstarter allows AI art but requires a public disclosure section, and board-game backers react strongly. UK Games Expo bars products made "completely or in significant part" with AI and does not define "significant". AI drafts finished by a person may still be refused there.
6. **Biggest game risk: art giving away secrets.** A customer's freshness type, amounts and prices are fixed by who they are. A face, a back or an edge colour that varies by card would reveal them. ART_DIRECTION.md §9 sets nine hard rules, including a fixed fruit order on customer backs (today's backs already make 9 of 36 customers unique).
7. **Fix before anything is printed:** the Drive print kit is still rules version 8, and RULES.md disagrees with the game on the generous customers and the guest orders.
8. **Cost:** nothing is spent without a quote and your approval. Estimates: 45 card portraits by a human illustrator about USD 4,500 to 36,000, plus USD 1,000 to 6,000 for a box cover. The recommended route (AI drafts plus a person's finishing) is about USD 2,250 to 6,750 for 45 portraits, plus the tool and the box. All of this is far above the RM500-a-month ceiling.

## 3. What exists today

- **All art is drawn in code.** Fruit, icons, props and 50 people are text grids turned into small pictures. Portraits are 16 × 16 pixels from one shared face, told apart by hair, accessory, skin and shirt (DEC-041). There are no image files to replace.
- **Swapping art is clean.** Every screen asks for a portrait through one function (`Face` in `src/ui/art.tsx`), and names, quirks, perks and freshness types live separately in `data.ts`. A new portrait source would need no change to rules, saves or online play.
- **The 25 collectible looks** are 11 wearables drawn as pixel rows on the 16 × 16 face, plus 14 stall pieces. Wearables only work on the pixel face, which is why Look E keeps them there.
- **Print is thin and partly outside the repo.** Only the card-back script is in the repo (63 × 88 mm, no bleed, no crop marks, RGB). Customer, shopkeeper, mat and token pages were generated elsewhere. They are rules version 8 and lack the freshness type.
- **Printed portraits are small:** about 21 mm (customers) and 26 mm (shopkeepers), drawn from 16 × 16 pixels. They are the weakest asset for a polished Kickstarter box.
- **Your recorded taste:** you chose a smooth toy-box look (DEC-021, "go with plan b"), then moved back to pixel art twice (DEC-023, DEC-033 "Look C"), and praised the current look ("artstyle is awesome i love it", DEC-057). That is why Look E keeps pixel art on the board and shows every new design beside today's look before anything is built (DEC-042).
- **Precedent for AI art:** the lorry picture was made with Gemini, and you accepted that AI-made art may not be protected by copyright (history, PLAN-011). It was later replaced by pixel art for consistency (DEC-052).

## 4. The two directions, scored

- **The Great Fruit Exchange alone:** strong shelf appeal and a perfect match for lorries, events and the box. But many historic crate labels and fruit ads drew people as racist or "exotic" caricatures. Copying real labels risks copyright and trademark claims: brands such as Sunkist and Chiquita appear to still use their marks, though no trademark search has been run. Detailed litho texture also blurs at phone sizes.
- **Orchard & Oddballs alone:** good personality and social energy. But "charming imperfection" is what AI tools keep least consistent. It slides toward exaggerated faces (against DEC-018 and DEC-041), and without a strong graphic frame it looks like generic mobile-game art.
- **Hybrid:** the Exchange supplies the graphics as code templates (no image generator), so every frame and edge is identical. Oddballs supplies the attitude, expressed only through pose, prop and expression. One flat style ties them together. Its one weakness is that each person has two renderings, and a shared character spec keeps them in step.

## 6. Pilot characters

**Recommended: Benny (shopkeeper, Big Basket) + Sailor Jia Wen (customer, card 25).**

| | Benny | Sailor Jia Wen |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Shopkeeper; public perk; computer rival in the daily market | Everyday customer; wants 2 apples (top $6) and 2 mangoes (top $10) |
| Shape | Broad, round, bearded, steady | Slim, upright, quick |
| Colour | Warm brown shirt, skin tone c | Harbour navy, skin tone a |
| Head | Short dark hair and beard (a clean base for hats later) | Long black hair; the bible will propose a sailor's cap as her one accessory |
| Prop | The big basket (his perk is public, so showing it is fine) | A rolled "mango map" (her quirk), not coins, so no money cue |

- **Why Benny:** a shopkeeper appears on the player card, box, Kickstarter page, app avatar and character pick. His broad build tests the bust template. His perk gives a natural prop, and his simple head makes the cleanest anchor for wearable looks later.
- **Why Jia Wen:** she carries the identity of "a harbour where the whole world comes to shop". Her back never singles her out. On today's backs (fruits in card order) it matches one Premium and one Bargain card. With the fixed order it would match two Premium, one Bargain and one other Everyday card. Her portrait tests the process: a prop chosen with no money cue, and the cue-balance check. She is Everyday and not generous in either list.
- **Contrast:** man and woman, broad and slim, warm and cool colours, bare head and cap, a shopkeeper's hero card and a small customer window.
- **What the pair does not cover:** guest cards, event-card people and the face-down walk-in figure. The walk-in figure is specified in the Character Bible.
- **Runners-up:** Rosa + Grandpa Tan (Rosa's bun and glasses are the hardest test for hats and the monocle). Raj + Grandpa Tan (the deepest skin tone tests printing and small sizes). Note: Tan's thrift quirk cannot be drawn literally until you decide its text, and his token has two accessories, so choosing him means settling both first.
- **Not a pilot yet:** Rara Jenab. She is your own approved design and a strong campaign face, but as a Premium card her back is unique today (section 11, item 4).

## 9. AI, copyright and Kickstarter

All outside facts here come from web search summaries. Fetching the primary pages was blocked in this session, so each must be re-checked on its source page, with a date, before a decision depends on it.

- **Kickstarter** allows AI content but requires a public "Use of AI" section: which tools, which parts are AI, which are original, and whether the owners of the source works gave consent. Hiding AI use can get a project suspended.
- **Backers:** board-game buyers are among the most hostile audiences. Reported examples: review-bombing of an AI cover on BoardGameGeek (March 2026), UK Games Expo barring products made "completely or in significant part" with AI (from September 2026; "significant" undefined), BackerKit barring content made only by AI, and award and publisher bans. Observational studies of Kickstarter campaigns (all categories, 2022 to 2024) link AI disclosure to clearly lower funding. Reported effects range from about 40% less money to about half the odds of reaching the goal.
- **Copyright:** the US Copyright Office (January 2025) says prompts alone do not make you the author; human changes, and the selection and arrangement of material, can be protected. Separately, the courts confirmed that an AI cannot be an author (Thaler; the Supreme Court declined to hear it in March 2026). The UK may remove its computer-generated-works rule. Malaysia's Copyright Act 1987 has no AI provision and no court cases yet, and a consultation ran in 2026. Raw AI images may give no exclusive rights, so copycats could reuse them.
- **Tool terms:** most tools give you whatever rights exist in the output. Protection against infringement claims is offered mainly on business or API plans (for example Adobe Firefly for enterprise, OpenAI's business and API plans, Google Vertex), with conditions to check. Consumer plans (about USD 10 to 50 a month) do not include it. Midjourney offers none found, is being sued by major studios, and shows lower-tier images in a public gallery, so it is not recommended for shipped art.
- **Recommended policy:**
  - AI for ideas and drafts.
  - A person finishes every shipped character picture.
  - The box and key art come from a named, credited artist with no AI drafts. Without budget, use a type-and-frame box instead of an AI picture.
  - Frames and graphics are made as code templates.
  - A manifest for every asset.
  - A full, honest "Use of AI" section, which also mentions that an AI coding assistant wrote the template code and the existing pixel art.
  - If showing at conventions such as UK Games Expo matters, the final character art needs to be made by a human artist, not only finished by one.

## 11. Problems found on the way (not art; for later)

1. **Generous customers disagree:** RULES.md lists 12 (including John, Grace and Lakshmi). The game computes 13 (including Daniel, Siti, Anna and Marcus). Checked by recomputing from `data.ts`.
2. **Guest orders disagree, and RULES.md disagrees with itself.** Its event table matches the game (Influencer 2 mangoes at $12, Gym Club 3 blueberries at $8, Hotel Chef 2/1/1, all at fixed prices). Its guest-card table says 3 at $11, 4 blueberries and 3/2/2. Its next line says the event's price change still applies, but the game treats guest prices as fixed.
3. **The Drive print kit is rules version 8:** no freshness type, the old "Not so" wording, old prices.
4. **Card backs list fruits in each card's own order,** in the printed kit, the app and the online server. That makes 9 cards unique from the back, including Premium Rara Jenab. A fixed fruit order would leave 4. (Separately, the fruit set alone already settles the type for 19 of 36 cards. That is how the rules work and is not changed here.)
5. **The online leak check** tests only the amounts and prices on face-down walk-ins. It does not test that the name, freshness type or generous flag are hidden. Only the server's allow-list protects them today, and Look E's app rule depends on the name staying hidden.
6. **The face-down "shopper" face** looks like Benny (same short hair and brown shirt).
7. **Likely bug (read in code, not reproduced):** online, any favour card a rival plays is announced as "Priority!" (`src/ui/game/animator.tsx` with the server's hidden-favour placeholder).
8. **Cast leftovers:** a fox sprite, 13 unused creature and costume parts, "animal traders" comments in `data.ts`, the creative bible's identity line still listing vampires and mermaids (DEC-039 removed creatures), and 11 sprites with two accessories against the one-accessory rule.
9. **Misleading quirks:** Bargain Wei Jie inspects fruit with a magnifier, Bargain Mei Ling splashes sellers of soggy fruit, Everyday Zul "wants fresh fruit".
10. **Store, launcher and splash art** use an older, off-palette style. No font licence or credit file exists.
11. **STATE.md was out of date** (it said 1.2.0 was live; the release records say 1.7.0 was published on 2026-11-07). Its top section is updated.

Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 need small plans of their own. Each changes text, app or test code, so none is inside PLAN-048.
