**Method.** I installed the repo's dependencies from the lockfile in the local clone (no new dependency) and ran simulated games through the real engine: 300 games per setting for the baseline, 200 for each what-if, all computer shops at Normal, Standard 10 days with events, specialities and unripe fruit on. **Baseline (today's rules, 10 days)**

| Shops | First 3 days: customers/day | Last 3 days: customers/day | Fill rate first / last | Shop turns short of their buy limit, first / last | Shop turns with an empty market, first / last |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2.1 | 4.9 | 57% / 36% | 4% / 32% | 0% / 10% |
| 3 | 3.5 | 6.9 | 52% / 32% | 9% / 36% | 3% / 17% |
| 4 | 4.9 | 8.9 | 52% / 30% | 12% / 35% | 5% / 22% |

(Fill rate = fruit sold divided by fruit the customers wanted.)

**What this shows (4 shops)**
- **Demand nearly doubles.** Customers per day go from 4.9 to 8.9; the fruit they want goes from about 19 to about 35.
- **Fruit sold per day stays flat** at about 14 from day 4 on. Supply does not grow with demand.
- **Market Days starve the market.** On days 3, 6 and 9 shops find an empty market on 15%, 41% and 49% of their turns (units on the market at a shop's turn start fall to 6.0, 3.5 and 2.7, against 13 to 18 on other days). Fill rate on day 9 is 12%.
- **Why demand grows:** one extra walk-in in the second half, one extra on each Market Day, and unserved customers coming back the next day. About 7 to 8 customers a day go unserved late in the game.
- **Quick (8 days, 4 shops):** the same pattern: last 3 days 8.7 customers a day, 33% fill, 33% of turns short, 21% empty.

**What-ifs (4 shops, 10 days, last 3 days)**

| Variant | Customers/day | Fruit wanted/day | Fill rate | Turns short | Turns with an empty market | Empty on days 3 / 6 / 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 8.9 | 35.5 | 30% | 35% | 22% | 15% / 41% / 49% |
| A: lorry also comes on Market Days | 7.7 | 28.4 | 59% | 8% | 2% | 1% / 2% / 2% |
| D: +1 of each fruit in the second half | 8.7 | 34.0 | 37% | 25% | 17% | 15% / 41% / 39% |
| C: no extra walk-in in the second half | 6.8 | 25.8 | 41% | 31% | 17% | 15% / 37% / 38% |
| A + D | 7.1 | 26.4 | 71% | 2% | 0% | 1% / 2% / 0% |
| A + C | 5.4 | 20.0 | 75% | 2% | 0% | 1% / 2% / 0% |

**Reading the what-ifs**
- **A alone fixes most of the supply problem** and also lowers late demand (7.7 vs 8.9), because fewer customers are left waiting. It is the simplest to play by hand: a farmer card every day.
- **D and C each help only a little** because they leave the Market Day starvation in place.
- **A + C** makes late-game demand level with early-game (5.4 vs 5.0 customers a day) and gives the highest fill rate; it removes one extra customer per day in the second half, a printed number.
- **Untested idea:** a half delivery on Market Days (keeps some scarcity). It needs an extra rule, so I did not test it.
- **Trade-off for A:** Market Days lose their "nothing arrives today" tension and keep only the extra walk-in and Last Call. That is a design call for you.
