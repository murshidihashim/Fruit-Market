## 1. The short version

1. **The core game is sound.** Seats are fair, the rules text matches the cards and numbers, and the basic game (no shopkeepers, favours or upgrades) is the tightest and fairest version.
2. **The extras are where the balance goes wrong.** Upgrades, shopkeeper perks and favour cards triple the number of lopsided games (11% in the basic game, 33% in the full game, 3 players).
3. **A rules mismatch:** the written rules, the set-up text and How to play all say "build one upgrade after each Market Day". The app lets a player build several in one evening. Skipping upgrades costs a player about $20 and nearly always loses (5.8% wins where 33% is fair).
4. **Two shopkeepers are off:** Big Basket (Benny) wins about 10 points above fair, Haggler (Rosa) about 9 points below. Extra starting cash does not help any perk, so cash perks do not work.
5. **Mango is the best fruit by 40%** (profit per fruit $5.30 against $3.7 for the others).
6. **Fruit almost never rots** when computers play (under 0.2 fruit per player per game). The headline tension, "sell it before it spoils", is not biting. Only human playtests can say whether this is true for people.
7. **Quick match is a 2-player game, the most lopsided size** (47% of games decided by $20 or more).
8. **The sales payout looked wrong on screen.** Totals jumped to their final value at the bell while customers were still being replayed. Fixed in a local branch (section 6).
9. **For a browser-first launch** the site has no install support (no manifest or service worker), no link-preview tags and no security headers. All three are small jobs.
10. **The printed kit cannot run the upgrade draft**: there are no upgrade cards, no day track and no queue strip, and the stall sheet cannot settle the tie-break (fruit sold).

## 2. How the simulation was run

- Every game is played start to finish by the computer players, using the same rules code as the app and server (all phases: buying, stocking, bell, rush, evening, night).
- **Baseline:** 3,000 games for each of 2, 3 and 4 players. Standard 10-day game, all players Hard, a different random shopkeeper for each stall (as at a table). Seats are fair, so any gap is the game, not the seat.
- **Other runs:** 500 to 1,500 games each. Game lengths, rule options, favour-card removal, upgrade policies, computer skill, and 11 changed-rule versions.
- **Reading the numbers:** win rates in 3-player games carry about +/-1.7 points of noise (500 games: +/-2.5). Treat gaps under 3 points as noise.
- **Limits:** these are computers, not people. They never play the Scout favour card, they buy only what they can sell, and their upgrade choices were tuned to the current app behaviour. Use the numbers to find structure, then confirm with human games.
- The test tooling was temporary and is not in the repo.

## 4. Rules and tabletop audit

### Found now

1. **Upgrades per evening do not match the written rules.** RULES.md ("draft one stall upgrade"), the set-up screen ("build one") and How to play say one. The engine has no limit and the evening panel says "you may build upgrades". Fixing the app to one per evening is a one-line rule check, plus retuning the computer players. Measured effect: lopsided games 33% to 27%, never-build penalty -$20 to -$10, fruit spoiled 0.08 to 0.33. Decision needed (section 8).
2. **The stall sheet has no "fruit sold" tally**, but the end-of-game tie-break is "whoever sold more fruit". Add a tally row.
3. **The printed kit is missing pieces the rules use:**
   - upgrade cards (4) and a draft procedure;
   - a day track with the Market Days and Closing Day marked;
   - the queue strip (dawn line-up) and the waiting pile area;
   - the fruit clock marker and day chips (the README says upgrade and Tip Jar tokens are "not printed yet");
   - a one-page crier card (the crier's top-minus-1 / top-minus-2 table) for the person who reads customers aloud.
4. **No solo or 2-player table rules** for a stall run by a card deck. The app has computer players; a table does not.
5. **No play-time estimate** for a table game. Time it at the first table test.
6. **Teaching ladder:** the simulation says the basic game is the fairest. Suggest teaching in four steps: basic, plus shopkeepers, plus favours, plus upgrades. The Playtest Pack could say so.
7. Stale code comment: `src/engine/game.ts` still says "rules version 4".

### Checked and fine

Every count and number in RULES.md matches the app: 20 delivery, 36 customer, 13 event and 12 favour cards; start cash, shelf, buy limits, upgrade costs, jam price, Closing Day bonus, tip, generous customers list; Market Day days; tie-break order; walk-in counts.

## 5. Suggested balance changes (all need approval; effects are from the simulation)

| # | Change | What the simulation showed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Upgrades: one per Market Day** (as written), and retune the computer to take the Handcart first | Lopsided 33% to 27%; median margin $13 to $11; never-build penalty -$20 to -$10; rot 0.08 to 0.33. Average cash -5% | Matches every printed rule |
| B2 | **Mango costs $1 more** (fresh $5, green $4) | Mango profit/fruit $5.30 to $4.57 (others $3.7); cash -4%; nothing else moves | $2 more would fully level it |
| B3 | **Big Basket: the 5th piece costs $1 more** | Basket wins 43% to 39% (fair 33%); nothing else moves | Try $2 for fair |
| B4 | **Rework Haggler, Nest Egg and Charmer so they give power, not cash** | Nest Egg at $10 start: 31.7% to 31.1% (no change). Haggler with $2 start: +2 points. Haggler's price counting $1 lower: no help | Cash is not scarce, so cash perks do nothing. Ideas, not yet simulated: Haggler "your first sale each day pays +$2"; Nest Egg "10-fruit shelf" |
| B5 | **Keep the poorest-first buying order** | A rotating order doubles lopsided games (33% to 54%), halves close games (17% to 8%) and makes leaders win | The cost: an early lead means nothing (day-5 leader wins at chance) |
| B6 | **2 players: add one walk-in** | Cash +45%; median margin unchanged in dollars, so 30% smaller as a share of cash | Does not cure lopsided games (47%) |
| B7 | **Quick match and new players: offer the Basic game** | Basic game: lopsided 11% (3p) / 21% (2p) against 33% / 47% | Easiest fairness win |
| B8 | **Cold Room and Juice Bar need a real job** | Neither changes win rate by more than +/-2 under any rule tested | Rot is rare, so insurance upgrades are worthless. Make them capacity or pricing upgrades |

**About rot:** the rules say fruit goes bad, but computers lose under 0.2 fruit a game. Before changing any rule to make rot bite, record fruit spoiled and unsold per human game in the anonymous stats and look at a week of real play.

### The payout change (U2)

- **What was wrong:** the game works out and pays every sale in one step at the bell (`resolve` in `src/engine/game.ts`). The screen drew that final cash straight away while the customers were replayed, so the numbers never built up.
- **What changed:** the cash chips and the rank badge are held back at their before-sales amount, and each customer's payment is added when its coins land, counting up a coin at a time. A Tip Jar tip lands at the end. Display only, no rule change.
- **Checked:** type check, lint, the whole test suite (174 tests, 3 new, including one that proves the held-back amounts equal what each stall had before the sales in 20 full games) and the build pass. In a real game the recorder showed the totals climbing in separate steps as each customer paid. Not yet tried online with a second player or on a phone.
- **Where:** commit `3394969` on branch `feat/payout-suspense`. Nothing is pushed.

## 7. Game modes, technology and operations

### Modes today

Solo and pass-and-play (2 to 4 stalls), private online rooms, quick match (2 players), Daily market, Market League (6 stages, win to advance), Wardrobe and 43 achievements.

### Mode ideas, best first

1. **Table Companion** (the app as the crier, bank and clock for a real table game). It uses the same rules code and fits the tabletop plan. It would also fix the table's biggest chore.
2. **League and Daily with objectives and stars** ("reach $120 with no upgrades", "sell 10 mangoes"), which adds goals without new rules.
3. **Daily with a twist of the day** (a rule option on rotation).
4. **Practice coach** (hints, "what would a good player do"), for new players.
5. **2 against 2** teams online; **async rooms** (a move a day); **replay links**.
