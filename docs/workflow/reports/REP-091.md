## 1. What changed for players

- **Fill your crate.** At Stocking your shelf slides up, drawn like the Wholesale Market: one column per fruit, newest at the top, tinted by freshness. Every space you hold has − and +. Each + sends that piece flying into your crate; the plate counts up. The crate starts from the suggested pieces (the planner's counts, oldest first).
- **Rule (rules version 12).** Only the pieces in your crate can sell, oldest of them first, and only if fresh enough for the customer's price. Before, the crate was a number and the oldest fresh-enough pieces came from the whole shelf. Computer players fill their crates with their oldest pieces.
- **Pricing (DEC-076).** Equal wooden crates; tap a fruit in your crate to price it with a price bubble (−/+ and quick prices with their buyer counts); a demand board (pieces wanted, pieces out, buyers at each price); "The Bell" pop-up on Day 1 and from the i button.
- **Animation.** The rivals' crates drop onto the table with swinging name plates, their lids lift as pricing starts, the demand board drops in, the price bubble pops. The existing "Stocking" and "The Bell" banners and the rush pop-up stay. A separate ringing-bell moment before the rush was in the prototype but is not built.

## 3. Risks

- People can now keep fresh fruit back and put tired fruit in their crate; a Premium buyer then buys nothing from them. The suggested crate (oldest first) can include tired pieces, so a careless player may sell less than before.
- Older apps and unfinished rules 11 games are refused, as with every rules change.
