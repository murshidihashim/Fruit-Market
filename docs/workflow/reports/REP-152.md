# REP-152 · A more skill-based, more competitive game

| | |
|---|---|
| Game | Fruit Market 1.31.5 live (rules version 20) |
| Report date | 2026-11-28 08:50 (Asia/Kuala_Lumpur) |
| Type | Design direction (planning only; nothing in the game changes) |
| Request | Owner, 2026-11-28: "i need the game to be more skill based and more competitive" |
| Approval | None asked for. So far the owner likes D of REP-149 (favour cards sold by secret bid) |

This narrows REP-149 to REP-151 to one aim. Instead of another list, it says where skill and competition are missing today, and the few changes that would add the most of each.

## 1. Where the game stands (computer games, rules 20)

**Skill already counts for something.** A Hard computer beats a Normal one in 72% of 2-stall games and wins 65% at 3 stalls (33% would be chance). A Hard computer beats an Easy one 87% of the time. (`npm run balance`, 150 games a test.)

**But the contest is thin.** From REP-149:

| What | Today | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Orders where two or more stalls are selling | 27% to 47% | In most sales there is no rival to beat |
| Pieces sold within $1 of the customer's top price | 73% to 87% | The "right" price is usually obvious |
| Favour cards | Drawn at random, about 1 a game | A lucky card, not an earned one |
| Upgrades | Everyone can build any; skipping them costs $4 | No fight, little effect |
| Walk-in customers | Face down until Stocking | You buy fruit partly blind |
| Cash from Day 5 | Idle ($80 by Day 10) | Nothing to outbid a rival for |

**What "skill" and "competitive" need.** Skill needs decisions where a better player chooses differently from a weaker one, and luck that is the same for everyone. Competition needs stalls to fight over the same thing, often, and a result that says who played better.

## 2. The changes that would add the most

### S1. Put more fruit on the table, so every sale is a price duel
- **What.** Bigger lorries (about 3 more pieces a day at 3 stalls) and a buying limit of 5. No new rule.
- **Why.** Fruit is short today, so most orders have one seller who charges the top price. With more fruit, most orders have two or three sellers: reading rivals and pricing $1 better becomes the whole game, and overbuying means fruit that spoils. This is the single biggest lever for both skill and competition, because it makes the game's own central contest happen more often.
- **Target.** Orders with two or more sellers above 60% (27% to 47% today); pieces sold near the top price under 60%; 2 to 3 pieces spoiled per stall a game; a Hard computer beating a Normal one more often than today.
- **Care.** Pure balance work, measured before anything ships.

### S2. Replace luck with bidding
- **Favour cards by secret bid** (D, already liked): the card goes to the stall that judged its worth best, not the one that happened to draw it.
- **One of each upgrade, by secret bid** (REP-150, 2): five upgrades, five owners at most, so stalls must fight for the one they need.
- **Why.** Both turn a random or free thing into a judgment against rivals, and give cash a job.

### S3. Show the whole crowd, so buying is reasoning, not guessing
- **What.** Walk-ins are face up while stalls buy (today they show only their fruit).
- **Why.** Every stall then knows exactly what will be wanted and for how much. What is left unknown is only what rivals will bring and charge, which is the part skill can work on. Luck from the deal is the same for all.
- **Care.** Gossip, Scout and the Sharp Ears perk exist to peek at walk-ins; they would need new jobs. Could be a "competitive rules" switch rather than the standard game.

### S4. One mind-game at the bell
- **Haggle chips** (REP-151, 10): buy a chip in advance; after prices flip, move one price by $1. Rewards the player who foresaw a close fight.
- or **The chalkboard boast** (REP-150, 7): claim "cheapest apples" in public; +$1 a piece if true, pay $2 if not. Rewards reading rivals and bluffing.
- **Why.** Either adds a decision where good players will clearly beat weak ones. One, not both.

### S5. Competitive play around the game (no rule change)
- **Rated quick match.** A rating that goes up and down with results, shown beside your name, and matches made between close ratings.
- **Duel on the same deal.** Challenge a friend: both play the same shuffled game against the same computer stalls, and the higher cash wins. All luck is shared, as in the Daily market, so only play differs.
- **Pick and ban shopkeepers** before a rated match, instead of first come.
- **Best of three** in private rooms.
- **A move timer** for rated play.
- **Why.** These are what make a game feel competitive to players, whatever the rules are.
- **Care.** A rating needs a server record per player and rules against abuse (`server/meta.ts`, `server/limits.ts`): its own plan.

### S6. Teach the player what a better player would have done
- **What.** After each game, a short review: "Day 4: you sold apples at $4; $5 would still have won the sale (+$2)". "Day 6: 2 blueberries spoiled." Total: "You left $11 on the table."
- **Why.** Skill only feels like skill if you can see how to improve. No rule change at all.

## 3. What I would not do for this aim

- **More random extras** (the seconds box, mystery crates): more luck.
- **End-of-game sets and ribbons**: they add scoring, not contest.
- **Removing poorest-buys-first**: it is a catch-up rule, but it is the same for everyone and games stay open with it (the Day 5 leader wins about 55% at 3 stalls). If a pure contest is wanted later, buying order could be bid for.

## 4. The order I would take

| Step | What | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **S1** more fruit, measured | Biggest effect, no new rule; it also shows how much the other steps are still needed |
| 2 | **S2** favours and upgrades by secret bid | Already liked; replaces luck with judgment |
| 3 | **S6** the after-game review | Cheap, no rule risk, makes skill visible |
| 4 | **S5** rated quick match and duels | The competitive frame, once the rules reward skill |
| 5 | **S3** and **S4** | Stronger medicine; try as "competitive rules" first |

## 5. Decisions needed from the owner

1. Who is the competition: **other people online**, or **the computer and the Daily leaderboard**? It decides whether S5 (ratings, duels) or S1 to S4 and stronger computer players come first.
2. May I plan **S1** (more fruit, measured with computer games, no new rule) as the first package, with **S2** next?
3. Should the sharper rules (S3, S4) be the standard game, or a separate "competitive rules" switch that rated matches use?
