**How to read the scores.** 10 = ready to sell next to good commercial games of this type. 7 = solid, needs
polish. 5 = works, but a paying customer would notice gaps. 3 or less = not started or a serious blocker. Scores
are my judgement from the code, the test results and the records. **No unfamiliar player has played rules
version 4 yet**, and that caps every score about fun or balance.

## 3. Tabletop version

| Angle | Score | What's good | What's holding it back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rules completeness** | **5** | Full rules written (`RULES.md`), designed to work without an app | Written as a reference, not a rulebook: no set-up diagram, no worked example turn, no component list, no quick-reference card |
| **Upkeep at the table** | **2.5** | Simultaneous crates and prices keep downtime low | **Ageing by hand is the biggest problem.** Every night, every fruit in 4 shops plus the market moves one space: roughly 40–80 cube moves a night, about 10 times a game. Also: waiting customers with part-filled orders, Market Day and Last Call tracking, a five-step tie order |
| **Balance for people** | **3.5** | The app's computer tests give a starting point | No paper playtest has ever happened; computer results don't show how people bluff, read and undercut |
| **Components and production** | **1.5** | Card counts are known (20 farmer, 24 customer, 13 event, 12 favour, 6 trait, 4 speciality) | No prototype; no card or board art; no price dials or screens designed; no manufacturing quote, retail price or box size |
| **Table presence and theme** | **4.5** | A colourful fruit market would look great on a table | Nothing designed yet for the physical look |
| **Play time and player count** | **4** | 2–4 players; 8/10/14-day lengths | Real play time unknown (my guess is 60–90 minutes for 10 days, which is long for this weight); 2 players is the weakest count (42% blowouts) |

**Tabletop overall: 3 / 10.**

**The ageing fix to test first.** Replace "move every fruit each night" with **date-stamped crates**. When you buy a
fruit, place it on the day track at the day it goes off (for example, an apple bought on day 3 goes on day 10). Nothing
moves at night; at dawn you clear the crate for today, and freshness is read from how many days are left. This keeps
the same rule and removes almost all upkeep. It needs your approval as a rule change.

---

## 4. The biggest risks, in order

1. **Nobody has played rules version 4.** Every balance and fun score here is a guess until real people play it.
2. **The rules keep moving.** Three changes today. A game needs a frozen rule set to playtest, print and sell.
3. **Tabletop upkeep (nightly ageing)** would put off physical players.
4. **Balance gaps:** Big Basket, dead specialities, 2-player blowouts.
5. **Commercial basics are missing:** name or trademark check, store art, iOS, monetisation, a backup plan.
6. **Online population:** quick match will be empty at launch without an async mode or friends invites.

---

## 5. Recommended path to publish both

| Step | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Freeze the rules** for a playtest round; fix only bugs | Stable target for testing |
| 2 | **Digital playtest:** 5 or more new players on the preview; watch, don't help | The only way to learn whether the bell is fun |
| 3 | **Paper prototype** with date-stamped crates; play at least 5 times | Proves the tabletop works and gives balance data from people |
| 4 | **Balance pass** from both playtests (Big Basket, specialities, 2 players) | Fix what people actually hit |
| 5 | **Name, art and audio direction:** trademark search, key art, card frames, recorded sound | Needed for the store page and the box |
| 6 | **Digital launch prep:** real-phone tests, backups, monetisation decision, store assets, then a soft launch | Release-ready evidence |
| 7 | **Tabletop production prep:** rulebook with examples, component list, print-and-play version, manufacturing quote | Before any crowdfunding or print run |

A sensible order is **digital first**. It is the cheapest way to test the rules and gather players, and the app can
later teach the physical game.
