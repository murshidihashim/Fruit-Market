## D. Standing orders (contracts)

Background facts used below. An order wants 3 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 5 pieces at 2 to 6 stalls. Ceiling and fine: blueberry $9 / $6, watermelon $12 / $8, apple $11 / $7, mango $18 / $12. The whole order code is `game.ts` 1117-1127 (offers), 1140-1141 (order of stalls), 1169-1183 (fine); the screen forecast `rowStock` (962-982) agreed with the real rush in 2,000 random crates (`i-out.txt` I2).

### D1. Shortage and rival allocation

**Engine** (`d-out.txt`):
- **D1a.** Watermelon order, 3 pieces, bid $8. Holder P0 put out 1 new Fresh. P1 put out 1 new Fresh + 1 Okay; P2 put out 2 older Fresh. Result: P0 1 at $8; **P1 2 at $7, including its Okay piece; P2 nothing.** Fine 2 x $8 = $16. The engine picks the rival whose single freshest piece is youngest and takes everything it needs from that rival (freshest first) before it looks at the next rival (`game.ts` 1140: holder, then `freshness`, then buying order; `freshness` is the rival's freshest usable piece, line 1126).
- **D1b.** Two rivals both have a new Fresh piece: the one earlier in today's buying order is taken first (2 pieces), the other gives 1. Swapping the buying order swaps the result.
- **D1c.** A rival whose only pieces are Soft: nothing is taken (Soft is skipped, line 1121). **D1d.** Nobody has any: no sale. In both the holder (cash $30) paid 3 x $8 = $24.
- **D1e.** The holder put out 5 watermelons (2 new Fresh, 1 older Fresh, 1 Okay, 1 Soft): the order took the **three Fresh**; the row was left the Okay and the Soft. This is the reverse of the row rule (oldest first).
- **D1f.** Only the crate counts: 3 on the shelf, 1 in the crate -> 1 supplied, short 2, fined $16.
- **D1g.** Rival pay is bid - $1, never under $1: bid $2 -> $1; **bid $1 -> $1** (the same as the holder).
- A rival's posted price is ignored: in D1a P1 had posted $30 and was still taken at $7.

**Written.** RULES.md 148: "it buys the freshest pieces **rivals** have put out and pays them $1 less than the holder's bid (never less than $1)". Guidebook line 370 says the same; the order card (line 228) omits "never less than $1". All three agree with each other, **but not with the engine in D1a**: read literally, "the freshest pieces rivals have put out" would take P1's new Fresh and P2's two older Fresh, not P1's Okay piece. Nothing written says which rival goes first on a tie.

**Verdict.** Unclear text (the engine is self-consistent and its forecast matches). **Fix (text, both documents):** "If it still wants more, it goes to the rival whose freshest piece is youngest (tie: the earlier buyer today) and takes that rival's Fresh or Okay pieces, freshest first; then the next rival." Stall by stall is also easier at a table than comparing every piece. If the owner prefers the literal piece-by-piece reading, the engine (1117-1141 and `rowStock`) has to change instead.

### D2. Reserved pieces and the same stall's row customers; can a rival protect its fruit?

**Engine.**
- Holder: the order takes its freshest Fresh/Okay pieces first, up to the order size; only what is left (the older pieces) is offered to the row (D1e).
- Rival: same thing, at bid - $1. **D2b:** P1 put out 3 new Fresh + 2 Okay watermelons and posted $7 for Zara Zapp (top $7). The order (holder short 3) took the 3 Fresh at $7; the two Okay ones did not qualify at $7, so Zara Zapp left unserved and P1 kept two ageing pieces.
- **A rival can protect fruit only by leaving it on the shelf** (D2a: P1 kept 3 on the shelf, the order went to P2). Its price tag makes no difference (D1a). Soft pieces are safe in the crate.
- The holder cannot choose which pieces go to the order, cannot hold Fresh pieces back for the row except by keeping them out of the crate (and being fined), and cannot refuse.

**Written.** Guidebook reminder (line 412): "A standing order takes rivals' crate pieces whether they like it or not, at $1 under the winning bid." RULES.md says the order is served "from the crates". Agree with the engine.

**Verdict.** OK as written, but see D3: with a low winning bid this is a forced sale at $1.

**Buying days that work** (`m-out.txt`, real nights; order dealt day 3, due day 5):

True for apple and watermelon; **a blueberry order has two useful days, a mango order one** (the Market Day itself), unless ripe mangoes are left at the market ($6) or the holder has a Ripening Room. At 5 and 6 stalls a mango order wants 5 and the buying limit is 4. **Verdict: unclear text.** **Fix (text):** add "Blueberries bought today will be Soft by then; green mangoes must be bought today."

### D3. Fines

**Engine** (`game.ts` 1169-1183): the fine is charged **straight after the order is collected and before any row customer is served**, from the cash the holder has at that moment: dawn cash, minus any Health Inspector fine, minus this morning's purchases, plus what the order just paid it. `fine = min(cash, floor(short x fine / 2 if Order Book))`. Nothing is carried over.

- **D3a, the broke holder (exploit).** Blueberry order (fine $6 a piece), holder P0 won at **$1**. Due day: P0 has $12, buys 4 Fresh watermelons ($12), cash $0, puts no blueberries out. P1 has 3 Fresh blueberries out at $5 for Hadi Hammer (3 blueberries, top $5). Result: the order takes P1's three blueberries and pays P1 **$1 each ($3 for fruit that cost $6 and would have sold for $15)**; P0 owes $18 and **pays $0**; then P0 sells its watermelons for $21 and draws a favour card; Hadi Hammer leaves unserved.
