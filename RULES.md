# Fruit Market: Rules

*Game design by Murshidi bin Md Hashim. Tabletop edition rules (rules version 5).*

Fruit Market is a game about buying and selling fruit. Each player runs a small fruit stall that buys from the farmers and sells to the town. Fruit goes bad over time, so you have to turn it into cash fast. Every day ends with one question: **how much can I charge before my rival, or the rot, gets me?**

Every rule here can be played with cards and coins on a table; the app only does the bookkeeping.

## Goal

Have the **most cash** at the end of the last day. Fruit left on your shelf and specialities you built are worth nothing at the end. Ties go to whoever sold more fruit.

## Players and length

- 2 to 4 shops. Each one is played by you, a friend on the same phone, or a computer player (Easy, Normal or Hard).
- **Quick** is 8 days, **Standard** is 10 days and **Long** is 14 days.
- Each shop starts with **$10**, a shelf of 8 spaces, and no favour cards.

## The fruit

Every fruit uses the same freshness rule: it sells at the customer's full price when **fresh**, **$1 less when okay** and **$2 less when not so**. Fruits differ in how many nights they stay in each stage, and in price.

| Fruit | Play style | Track (oldest → freshest) | Cost: Fresh / Okay / Not so | Unripe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🫐 Blueberry | Cheap and popular, mushy in 3 days. Fast turnover. | not so · okay · fresh | $2 / $1 / $1 | none |
| 🍉 Watermelon | The steady seller. | not so · okay · okay · fresh · fresh | $3 / $2 / $1 | 1 night ($1) |
| 🍎 Apple | The keeper: lasts a week. Never the best price, never a loss. | not so · okay ×3 · fresh ×3 | $3 / $2 / $1 | none |
| 🥭 Mango | Buy green for $3, ripens in 2 nights, sells at a premium, spoils fast once ripe. | not so · okay · fresh · fresh | $4 / $2 / $1 | 2 nights ($3) |

Every night all fruit moves one space left. Fruit that falls off the left end spoils. Unripe fruit can't be sold yet and becomes fresh as nights pass.

## Cards

- **Farmer cards (20):** each lists three fruits, 3 to 5 of each, printed for 3 shops. The fruit not on the card arrives as 1. With **2 shops**, take 1 less of each listed fruit (minimum 1); with **4 shops**, add 1 of every fruit. Some cards mark one fruit as arriving unripe.
- **Customer cards (36):** a name and up to three fruits, each with an amount and the most they pay for a *fresh* one. The back of each card shows the fruits they want.
- **Event cards (13):** one is drawn each morning from Day 2 onward and shown a day early.
- **Favour cards (12):** a secret reward for serving a customer's whole order by yourself. Hold up to 3 (4 with the Charmer trait).

## The queue

Each morning shows tomorrow on the **Tomorrow strip**: the next farmer card, the next event, one **expected customer** face up, and the **walk-ins** face down. Walk-ins show only the fruit on their card backs.

- Walk-ins per day: **players − 2**, every day of the game.
- **Market Day:** one more walk-in (see below). The lorry still comes.

## A day in five beats

### 1. Dawn
1. Apply today's **event**.
2. The farmer's lorry unloads today's card at the freshest position (or the unripe position). The lorry comes every day, Market Days included.
3. Draw tomorrow's farmer card, event and queue for the Tomorrow strip.

### 2. Market
Each shop buys **up to 4 fruits** (5 with a Barrow), paying the cost for that fruit's freshness. Your shelf holds 8 (12 with a Barrow). **The poorest shop buys first** (ties go round the table; Day 1 is random). A Priority favour jumps the queue.

### 3. Open stalls
Today's customers arrive: yesterday's unserved customers (now **waiting, last day**), the expected customer and the walk-ins, who turn face up now. Each shop secretly chooses how many of each ripe fruit to put in its **for-sale crate**; then all crates are shown at once. Fruit you keep back stays on your shelf.

### 4. The bell
Each shop secretly sets **one price per fruit** it put out, then everyone reveals together.

- **Price cap:** a customer pays at most their card price for a fresh fruit, $1 less for okay and $2 less for not so. When you sell, your **oldest fruit that is fresh enough for your price** goes first.
- There is **no lower limit** (except on a Minimum Price day).

### 5. The rush
Customers buy in queue order: waiting customers first, then new ones. For each fruit a customer wants, they buy from the **cheapest stall** that has fruit fresh enough for its price; leftover demand **spills to the next stall**.

- **Ties:** Favoured Trade, then the Haggler, then fresher fruit, then whoever bought earlier today. (Today's buying order was set at dawn, poorest shop first. Cash is not used, because it changes while customers are paid.)
- You're paid as soon as you sell.
- **Favours:** serve a customer's entire remaining order by yourself and draw a favour card (at most one per day).
- A new customer who doesn't get everything comes back tomorrow as *waiting*. A waiting customer leaves after today either way.

### Evening and night
- **Evening:** play evening favours (Priority, Market Gossip). On the evening after each Market Day (days 3, 6, 9 and 12), draft **one stall speciality** from the four.
- **Night:** all fruit at the market and in every shop ages one step. Spoiled fruit is thrown away.

## Market rhythm

- **Market Days (days 3, 6, 9 and 12, never the last day):** one more walk-in. The lorry still comes.
- **Last Call (final day):** customers pay $1 more. Afterwards the **jam maker** buys every shop's leftover ripe fruit for $1 each.

Market Days and Last Call are on by default and can be switched off in the set-up.

## Stall specialities

Drafted on the evening after each Market Day. Each can be built once, and each changes what you do.

| Speciality | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| ❄️ Cold Room | $4 | Your fruit ages only every other night, and heatwaves can't touch it. |
| 🧃 Juice Bar | $3 | +$1 for each okay or not-so fruit you sell. Each night up to 3 fruits that would spoil become juice: $2 each. |
| 🛒 Barrow | $6 | Buy up to 5 fruits a day and hold 12. |
| 🪧 Premium Display | $8 | Your fresh fruit may be priced $1 above a customer's normal limit. |

## Characters

Before the game, each shop picks one of six characters. A character gives one perk for the whole game, and everyone can see it. **No two shops can be the same character.** Computer shops take the characters the people did not pick. On a table, lay the six character cards face up and pick in turn, starting with the player to the left of the first-player token. In the app, pick on the set-up screen, or when you enter an online room (a taken character is greyed out, and you can change yours in the lobby until the game starts).

| Character | Perk | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rosa | Haggler | You win tied prices, even against fresher fruit. |
| Benny | Big Basket | Buy 1 extra fruit every day. |
| Raj | Bargain Hunter | Your first fruit each day costs $1 less. |
| Chen | Charmer | Start with 3 favour cards, and hold up to 4. |
| Kiran | Local Favourite | Customers you serve completely by yourself tip you $4. |
| Saleh | Nest Egg | Start with $15 instead of $10. |

(Rules version 5. Before, each shop was dealt 3 traits on the first morning and kept one.)

## Playtest options

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| Core market | No characters, perks, favour cards or specialities: just buying, stalls and the bell. |
| Street prices (beginner) | Every customer pays one street price per fruit (fresh): blueberry $5, watermelon $7, apple $6, mango $9. Cards with the "generous" icon pay $2 more. |
| Market pulse (advanced) | After each day, a fruit that ran short gets a +$1 price marker (up to +$2); a fruit with a glut at the market gets −$1 (down to −$1); otherwise the marker drifts back to 0. |

## Playing on a table

The rules above are the same on a table and in the app. These are the ways to run them by hand (added in rules version 5; none of them changes a rule, apart from the tie-break line above).

**Order of customers.** Lay the customer cards in one row, always in this order: waiting customers (yesterday's unserved), the expected customer, the walk-ins in the order they were drawn, then an event customer such as the Hotel Chef. Serve them left to right.

**The reader.** One player reads the customers aloud and pays the shops from the bank, so shops never swap coins with each other. Use the player who bought last that morning. For each customer line the reader says the real top price for the day (card price plus any event, Last Call or Street Price change), then which fruit qualifies at each price: at **top − 2 or less any fruit** qualifies, at **top − 1 fresh or okay**, at **the top itself fresh only**, and above the top nothing (a Premium Display may go $1 over for fresh fruit). The cheapest posted price sells first, and each shop hands over its oldest piece that still qualifies.

**Order circles.** Every customer card has one printed circle for each unit it wants, grouped by fruit. A fruit piece that is sold sits on a circle. Empty circles are what the customer still wants, so a waiting customer simply keeps its pieces on the card.

**The sole-supplier cube.** The first shop to sell to a customer puts a cube of its colour on the card. If a second shop sells to the same customer, the cube comes off. When the order is complete, a cube left on the card means that shop served it alone: it draws a favour card (and a Local Favourite takes its $4 tip).

**Dawn line-up.** At dawn every shop puts its cash in a pile in front of it. Shop tokens line up on a queue strip, fewest coins first, ties starting from the first-player token. Today's buying order, the evening order and the last tie-break all use this row. A Priority card moves its owner to the front.

**Evening.** Keep favour cards face down in front of you, so everyone can count them. Only shops holding a card, or with a speciality on offer after a Market Day, take an evening turn, in line-up order.

**Day chips instead of moving fruit.** Fruit piles keep still. A *fruit clock* marker shows the clock number: it starts at 1 and moves on 1 each night (2 after a Heatwave night). Every pile of fruit carries a chip with the clock number on the day it became fresh. A pile's **age** is the clock number minus its chip. A fruit bought **unripe** gets the chip of the clock number on which it ripens (the clock number now plus 1 for watermelon, plus 2 for mango); it is green until the clock reaches that number.

| Age | Blueberry | Watermelon | Apple | Mango |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | fresh | fresh | fresh | fresh |
| 1 | okay | fresh | fresh | fresh |
| 2 | not so | okay | fresh | okay |
| 3 | spoiled | okay | okay | not so |
| 4 | | not so | okay | spoiled |
| 5 | | spoiled | okay | |
| 6 | | | not so | |
| 7 | | | spoiled | |

At dawn, throw away piles that have reached "spoiled". A Health Inspector counts the "not so" piles. A Juice Bar juices up to 3 fruits that spoil. A **Cold Room** shop has its own fruit clock that moves on only one night in two (the night after an even day) and never on a Heatwave night. When it buys fruit, it works out the age from the market clock and re-stamps the chip on its own clock. A **Fridge** adds 1 to the chips of that shop's ripe piles, never past today's clock number.

**Waiting.** Customers who are not fully served go to a waiting pile with their pieces. A waiting customer that is not fully served the next day leaves.

*Left out on purpose: a price ladder board for lining up posted prices. Sorting the price cards by hand works for 2 to 4 shops.*

## Balance notes

Rules version 5 (PLAN-020, REP-049 and REP-050). Rules version 4 (REP-031 to REP-033) had no lorry on Market Days and one more walk-in from halfway; in computer-player games that left the market empty on Market Days and nearly doubled the customers in the last three days. Rules 5 brings the lorry every day and drops the second-half walk-in. See REP-049 for the numbers (computer players only).

## Daily challenge

Everyone in the world plays the same shuffled decks each day: you against Rosa (Normal), Benny (Hard) and Chen (Normal) over 10 days. Scores are kept per rules version.

## What changed from the paper draft

| Paper draft | This edition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Players take turns undercutting in the open | Crates shown first, then one secret price per fruit revealed at the bell | One clear dramatic moment a day |
| Paid when a customer card leaves the market | Paid immediately on each sale | Simpler to follow |
| 2 rounds of the first-player token | Fixed 8, 10 or 14 days | Long enough to plan |
| Farmer card fruits only | The missing fruit still arrives (1) | Prevents days where nobody can sell anything |
| Event cards (6 types) | 13 events, each announced a day early | Events change decisions, not just numbers |
| First player rotates | Poorest shop buys first | Stops early leaders running away |
| (none) | Traits, specialities, Market Days, Last Call | Every game opens differently and has an arc |
