# Fruit Market: Rules

*Game design by Murshidi bin Md Hashim. Tabletop edition rules (rules version 9).*

Fruit Market is a game about buying and selling fruit. Each player runs a small fruit stall that buys from the farmers and sells to the town. Fruit goes bad over time, so you have to turn it into cash fast. Every day ends with one question: **how much can I charge before my rival, or the rot, gets me?**

Every rule here can be played with cards and coins on a table; the app only does the bookkeeping.

## The big idea

Fruit Market is a game of **timing and nerve**. Three things pull against you every day:

1. **The clock.** All fruit gets older every night, and old fruit sells for less and then not at all. Whatever you buy, you are racing to sell it.
2. **The secret price.** You never see your rivals' prices until the bell. Price too high and the customer walks past you; too low and you give money away.
3. **The rival's crate.** Everyone sees how many of each fruit every stall puts out, before prices are set. If a rival is short of apples, you know you can charge more.

You do not need to be the best at everything. Often the winner is simply the stall that threw away the least fruit and was brave one day when the others were not.

### One customer, start to finish

Professor Pebble wants **5 blueberries** and pays up to **$6** for a fresh one (so $5 for okay, $4 for tired).

- Stall A has 3 **fresh** blueberries, bought at $2 each, and posts **$5**.
- Stall B has 2 **okay** blueberries, bought at $1 each, and posts **$4**.
- Stall C has 4 **fresh** blueberries and posts **$6**.

Professor Pebble buys from the **cheapest stall** first. Stall B sells 2 at $4 (+$8, which cost $2). Professor Pebble still wants 3, so he moves to the next cheapest, Stall A, which sells 3 at $5 (+$15, which cost $6). His order is full, so Stall C sells nothing, even though its fruit was fresh. Because two stalls served Professor Pebble, nobody draws a favour card.

Had Stall A been alone in serving him, A would have drawn a favour card. That is the reason to be the one stall that has what a customer wants.

## Goal

Have the **most cash** at the end of the last day. Upgrades are worth nothing at the end. Fruit left on your shelf is worth only what the jam maker pays on Closing Day: $1 for each ripe piece, nothing for green fruit, and nothing if Closing Day is switched off. Ties go to whoever sold more fruit.

## Players and length

- 2 to 4 stalls. Each one is played by you, a friend on the same phone, or a computer player (Easy, Normal or Hard).
- **Quick** is 8 days, **Standard** is 10 days and **Long** is 14 days.
- Each stall starts with **$15**, a shelf of 8 spaces, and no favour cards.
- With **2 stalls** there is one walk-in a day (and the regular), so Gossip and Scout still have something to look at. Three or four stalls give a fuller market.

## The fruit

Every fruit uses the same freshness rule: it sells at the customer's full price when **fresh**, **$1 less when okay** and **$2 less when tired**. Fruits differ in how many nights they stay in each stage, and in price.

| Fruit | Play style | Track (oldest → freshest) | Cost: Fresh / Okay / Tired | Unripe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🫐 Blueberry | Cheap and popular, mushy in 3 days. Fast turnover. | tired · okay · fresh | $2 / $1 / $1 | none |
| 🍉 Watermelon | The steady seller. | tired · okay · okay · fresh · fresh | $3 / $2 / $1 | none |
| 🍎 Apple | The keeper: lasts a week. Never the best price, never a loss. | tired · okay ×3 · fresh ×3 | $3 / $2 / $1 | none |
| 🥭 Mango | Buy green for $4, ripens in 2 nights, sells at a premium, spoils fast once ripe. | tired · okay · fresh · fresh | $5 / $2 / $1 | 2 nights ($4) |

Every night all fruit moves one space left. Fruit that falls off the left end spoils. Unripe fruit can't be sold yet and becomes fresh as nights pass.

## Cards

- **Lorry cards (20):** ten named lorries, two copies of each. Every card prints how many blueberries / watermelons / apples / mangoes it delivers with **2, 3 and 4 stalls**; use the column for your table. Blueberry Bonanza, Orchard Run and Watermelon Wagon are loaded with one fruit, Quiet Delivery, Mixed Basket, Balanced Cart and Farmer's Choice are mixed loads, and the three mango lorries bring the most mangoes. **Mango Growers and Mango Season deliver their mangoes green** (unripe: they ripen on the shelf in two nights); every other lorry, Mango Rush included, delivers ripe mangoes.
- **Customer cards (36):** a name and up to three fruits, each with an amount and the most they pay for a *fresh* one. The back of each card shows the fruits they want. Each customer also has a **freshness type** (below), printed on the front. The type stays secret while a walk-in is face down.
- **Event cards (13):** one is drawn each morning from Day 2 onward and shown a day early. Mango Craze, Superfood Trend and Hotel Order bring a **guest card** (below).
- **Guest cards (3):** the Influencer, the Gym Club and the Hotel Chef. A guest joins the customers at the end of the row on the day of its event and is served like any other customer.
- **Favour cards (12):** a secret reward for serving a customer's whole order by yourself. Hold up to 3 (4 with the Charmer perk).

### Guest cards

| Guest | Event | Wants (fixed price for each) | Freshness type |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Influencer | Mango Craze | 2 mangoes at $12 | Premium (Fresh only) |
| The Gym Club | Superfood Trend | 3 blueberries at $8 | Everyday (Fresh or Okay) |
| The Hotel Chef | Hotel Order | 2 apples at $8, 1 watermelon at $8, 1 blueberry at $7 | Everyday (Fresh or Okay) |

A guest's price is fixed: the event's own price change is **not** added on top (on a Mango Craze the Influencer still pays at most $12 for a fresh mango). Only Closing Day adds its $1. As for anyone, okay fruit sells for $1 less and tired fruit for $2 less.

### Decks running out

- **Customers (36):** when the deck is empty, shuffle every customer card that is not on the table (not waiting, regular or walk-in) into a new deck. Most games never need it; a long 4-stall game sometimes does it once.
- **Favour cards (12):** played cards go to a discard pile. When the deck is empty, shuffle the discard pile into a new deck. Cards in hands stay there. If a stall's hand is full, it draws nothing.
- **Event cards (13):** a 14-day game uses all of them. If the deck is empty, shuffle all 13 again.
- **Delivery cards (20):** a game never uses more than 15.

## The queue

Customers are drawn a day ahead. Each morning, draw tomorrow's delivery card and tomorrow's event. When today's crowd turns face up (the moment buying ends), draw tomorrow's **regular** face up and tomorrow's **walk-ins** face down. Walk-ins show only the fruit on their card backs. On Day 1, draw the first regular and walk-ins during set-up. Together these make the **Tomorrow strip**.

- Walk-ins per day: **players − 2** every day of the game (but **1** with 2 stalls).
- **Market Day:** one more walk-in (see below). The lorry still comes.

## A day in five beats

### 1. Morning
1. Apply today's **event**.
2. The farmer's lorry unloads today's card at the freshest position (or the unripe position). The lorry comes every day, Market Days included.
3. Draw tomorrow's delivery card and event for the Tomorrow strip.

### 2. Buying
Each stall buys **up to 6 pieces of fruit** (8 with a Handcart), paying the cost for that fruit's freshness. Your shelf holds 8 (12 with a Handcart). **The poorest stall buys first** (ties go round the table; Day 1 is random). A Priority favour jumps the queue.

### 3. Stocking
Today's customers arrive: yesterday's unserved customers (now **waiting, last day**), the regular and the walk-ins, who turn face up now. Each stall secretly chooses how many of each ripe fruit to put in its **for-sale crate**, by setting a number on its stall sheet; then all crates are shown at once. The crate is a number: the pieces stay on your shelf, and fruit you keep back stays too.

### 4. The bell
Each stall secretly sets **one price per fruit** it put out, then everyone reveals together.

- **Price cap:** a customer pays at most their card price for a fresh fruit, $1 less for okay and $2 less for tired. When you sell, your **oldest fruit that is fresh enough for your price** goes first, taken from your whole shelf, up to the number in your crate.
- **Freshness type:** a *Premium* customer buys **Fresh fruit only**; an *Everyday* customer buys **Fresh or Okay**; a *Bargain* customer buys **Fresh, Okay or Tired**. A stall cannot sell fruit a customer will not take, whatever the price. Premium: Rara Jenab, Ravi Rocket, Captain Ahmad, Anna the Artist, Devi Bloom, Miss Cha-Cha (and the Influencer). Bargain: Magic Kumar, Professor Pebble, Postman Pip, Coral Mei, Rockstar Grace, Inspector Wei. Everyone else is Everyday (the Gym Club and Hotel Chef too).
- There is **no lower limit**. On a Price Controls day there is an **upper** limit for each fruit.

### 5. The rush
Customers buy in queue order: waiting customers first, then new ones. For each fruit a customer wants, they buy from the **cheapest stall** that has fruit fresh enough for its price; leftover demand **spills to the next stall**.

- **Ties:** Favoured Trade, then the Haggler, then fresher fruit (compare the age of the freshest piece each stall would hand over to this customer: the younger piece wins, so a piece of age 0 beats a piece of age 2 even though both are fresh), then whoever bought earlier today. (Today's buying order was set at dawn, poorest stall first. Cash is not used, because it changes while customers are paid.)
- You're paid as soon as you sell.
- **Favours:** serve a customer's entire remaining order by yourself and draw a favour card (at most one per day).
- A new customer who doesn't get everything comes back tomorrow as *waiting*. A waiting customer leaves after today either way.

### Evening and night
- **Evening:** play evening favours (Priority, Gossip). On the evening after each Market Day (days 3, 6, 9 and 12), build **one stall upgrade** from the four (one only, that evening).
- **Night:** all fruit at the market and in every stall ages one step. Spoiled fruit is thrown away.

## Market rhythm

- **Market Days (days 3, 6, 9 and 12, never the last day):** one more walk-in. The lorry still comes.
- **Closing Day (final day):** customers pay $1 more. Afterwards the **jam maker** buys every stall's leftover ripe fruit for $1 each.

Market Days and Closing Day are on by default and can be switched off in the set-up.

## Stall upgrades

Drafted on the evening after each Market Day. Each can be built once, and each changes what you do.

| Upgrade | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| ❄️ Cold Room | $4 | Your fruit ages only every other night, and heatwaves can't touch it. |
| 🧃 Juice Bar | $3 | +$1 for each okay or tired fruit you sell. Each night up to 3 pieces of fruit that would spoil become juice: $2 each. Tip: before the last day, $1 tired fruit makes $2 of juice. |
| 🛒 Handcart | $6 | Buy up to 8 pieces of fruit a day and hold 12. |
| 🪧 Premium Display | $8 | Your fresh fruit may be priced $1 above a customer's normal limit. |

## Shopkeepers

Before the game, each stall picks one of six shopkeepers. A shopkeeper gives one perk for the whole game, and everyone can see it. **No two stalls can be the same shopkeeper.** Computer stalls take the shopkeepers the people did not pick. On a table, lay the six shopkeeper cards face up and pick in turn, starting with the player to the left of the first-player token. In the app, pick on the set-up screen, or when you enter an online room (a taken shopkeeper is greyed out, and you can change yours in the lobby until the game starts).

| Shopkeeper | Perk | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rosa | Haggler | You win tied prices, even against fresher fruit, and your first sale each day pays $1 more. |
| Uncle Benny | Big Basket | Buy 1 extra fruit every day; the extra piece (the 7th, or the 9th with a Handcart) costs $2 more. |
| Raj | Bargain Hunter | Your first fruit each day costs $1 less. |
| Ah Chen | Charmer | Start with 4 favour cards, and hold up to 4. |
| Kiki | Tip Jar | Customers you serve completely by yourself tip you $4. |
| Saleh | Nest Egg | Start with $20 instead of $15, and buy first on day 3 (the first Market Day). |

(Rules version 5. Before, each stall was dealt 3 perks on the first morning and kept one.)

## Playing on a table

The rules above are the same on a table and in the app. These are the ways to run them by hand (added in rules version 5; none of them changes a rule, apart from the tie-break line above).

**Order of customers.** Lay the customer cards in one row, always in this order: waiting customers (yesterday's unserved), the regular, the walk-ins in the order they were drawn, then a special guest such as the Hotel Chef. Serve them left to right.

**The crier.** One player reads the customers aloud and pays the stalls from the bank, so stalls never swap coins with each other. Use the player who bought last that morning. For each customer line the crier says the real top price for the day (card price plus any event, Closing Day or Fixed prices change), then which fruit qualifies at each price: at **top − 2 or less any fruit** qualifies, at **top − 1 fresh or okay**, at **the top itself fresh only**, and above the top nothing (a Premium Display may go $1 over for fresh fruit). The cheapest posted price sells first, and each stall hands over its oldest piece that still qualifies. On a tie, the stall whose freshest handed-over piece is younger sells first.

**Money.** Use paper notes, not coins: in computer games a single stall peaked at $299 and all four together at $940. A bank of 60 × $1, 30 × $5, 20 × $10 and 10 × $50 is enough. The crier is the bank (see above), so stalls never swap notes with each other.

**The stall sheet and screen.** Each stall has one printed sheet with a shelf, a *crate* number box per fruit, and a *price* row per fruit ($1 to $16). Put a marker on a number to set it. Fold the card screen in front of the sheet so rivals cannot see. Set crate numbers, show them all at once, then set prices behind the screen and lift all screens together at the bell.

**Order circles.** Every customer card has one printed circle for each unit it wants, grouped by fruit. A fruit piece that is sold sits on a circle. Empty circles are what the customer still wants, so a waiting customer simply keeps its pieces on the card.

**Scout.** Turn one face-down walk-in card face up for yourself only, look, and turn it back. Say out loud that you played Scout.

**The sole-supplier cube.** The first stall to sell to a customer puts a cube of its colour on the card. If a second stall sells to the same customer, the cube comes off. When the order is complete, a cube left on the card means that stall served it alone: it draws a favour card (and a Tip Jar takes its $4 tip).

**First-player token.** On Day 1 give it to a random stall. It moves one seat clockwise every night.

**Dawn line-up.** At dawn every stall puts its cash in a pile in front of it. Stall tokens line up on a queue strip, fewest coins first, ties starting from the first-player token (on Day 1 the buying order is simply the seats from the token, not by cash). Today's buying order, the evening order and the last tie-break all use this row. A Priority card moves its owner to the front.

**Evening.** Keep favour cards face down in front of you, so everyone can count them. Only stalls holding a card, or with an upgrade on offer after a Market Day, take an evening turn, in line-up order.

**Two ways to track age.** On the printed mat, tokens slide one slot per night and each slot shows how fresh the fruit is. With day chips, the piles keep still. Both give the same ages. The rest of this section describes chips, which also work on a stall's own shelf.

**Day chips instead of moving fruit.** Fruit piles keep still. A *fruit clock* marker shows the clock number: it starts at 1 and moves on 1 each night (2 after a Heatwave night). Every pile of fruit carries a chip with the clock number on the day it became fresh. A pile's **age** is the clock number minus its chip. A fruit bought **unripe** (only mangoes arrive green) gets the chip of the clock number on which it ripens (the clock number now plus 2); it is green until the clock reaches that number.

| Age | Blueberry | Watermelon | Apple | Mango |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | fresh | fresh | fresh | fresh |
| 1 | okay | fresh | fresh | fresh |
| 2 | tired | okay | fresh | okay |
| 3 | spoiled | okay | okay | tired |
| 4 | | tired | okay | spoiled |
| 5 | | spoiled | okay | |
| 6 | | | tired | |
| 7 | | | spoiled | |

At dawn, throw away piles that have reached "spoiled". A Health Inspector, if one is in town, counts the "tired" piles at the start of the day, before anyone buys or throws fruit away. A Juice Bar juices up to 3 pieces of fruit that spoil. A **Cold Room** stall has its own fruit clock that moves on only one night in two (the night after an even day) and never on a Heatwave night. When it buys fruit, it works out the age from the market clock and re-stamps the chip on its own clock. A **Fridge** adds 1 to the chips of that stall's ripe piles, never past today's clock number.

**Waiting.** Customers who are not fully served go to a waiting pile with their pieces. A waiting customer that is not fully served the next day leaves.

*Left out on purpose: a price ladder board for lining up posted prices. Sorting the price cards by hand works for 2 to 4 stalls.*

## Balance notes

Rules version 8 (REP-086 and a second round of about 25,000 computer games): every shopkeeper now wins within about 3 points of a fair share (before, Big Basket won about 10 points more and Haggler about 9 points fewer). Haggler's first sale each day pays $1 more; Big Basket's extra piece costs $2 more (was $1); Nest Egg buys first on day 3; Charmer starts with 4 favour cards; two stalls get one walk-in a day, so a 2-player game is no longer decided by a single customer; and quick matches online have no upgrades. The Daily market also gets a small twist that rotates through the week (see below).

Rules version 9 (PLAN-047): every stall starts with $15 and buys up to 6 pieces a day (a Handcart 8). The lorry deck is ten named cards (two copies each) with the amounts for 2, 3 and 4 stalls printed on the card; Mango Growers and Mango Season arrive green. Customers have a freshness type (Premium, Everyday or Bargain). Customer quantities and prices were rebalanced (more watermelon, blueberry and apple, fewer mangoes asked; apples $1 lower and mangoes $1 higher). Event cards were rebalanced, Minimum Price became Price Controls (a maximum), and the Health Inspector fines at the start of the day. Nest Egg now starts with $20.

Computer players, still rules version 9 (PLAN-051, REP-089): the rules did not change, only how the app's computer players price. On Closing Day they now count each leftover ripe fruit at the jam maker's $1, and when they plan prices they follow the tie order above (Favoured Trade, then the Haggler, then the fresher fruit, then the earlier buyer). The rules version 8 aim, every shopkeeper within about 3 points of a fair share, is not met, and it was not met before this change either. In computer-player games (3 stalls, 10 days, 2,000 games for each shopkeeper at each level; fair is 33%): at Normal level the Haggler fell from about 41% to 37% and Big Basket rose from about 27% to 31%, but the Tip Jar now wins about 38%, about 4 points more than fair. At Hard level the Haggler still wins about 45%, 10 to 12 points more than fair, the same as before within chance, and Big Basket about 26%.

Rules version 7 (REP-086, computer-player simulation of about 40,000 games): the app now matches the printed rule of one upgrade per Market Day (before, several could be built in one evening, which made upgrades close to compulsory); a mango costs $1 more (fresh $5, green $4) because it earned about 40% more per fruit than the others; and Big Basket's extra piece costs $1 more because the perk won about 10 points more often than fair.

Rules version 6 (DEC-050): the Scout favour card replaces Good Riddance, which nobody wanted to play. The deck stays at 12 cards, two of each. Scout is information, like Gossip, but it covers one of today's walk-ins instead of all of tomorrow's.

Rules version 5 (PLAN-020, REP-049 and REP-050). Rules version 4 (REP-031 to REP-033) had no lorry on Market Days and one more walk-in from halfway; in computer-player games that left the market empty on Market Days and nearly doubled the customers in the last three days. Rules 5 brings the lorry every day and drops the second-half walk-in. See REP-049 for the numbers (computer players only).

## Daily market

Each day has a small twist, the same for everyone, rotating through seven: the usual game, a quick 8-day game, no upgrades, fixed prices, no events, a long 14-day game, and the basic game.

Everyone in the world plays the same shuffled decks each day: you against Rosa (Normal), Uncle Benny (Hard) and Ah Chen (Normal) over 10 days. Scores are kept per rules version, and per version of the app's computer players (scores are checked by replaying the game with them).

## What changed from the paper draft

| Paper draft | This edition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Players take turns undercutting in the open | Crates shown first, then one secret price per fruit revealed at the bell | One clear dramatic moment a day |
| Paid when a customer card leaves the market | Paid immediately on each sale | Simpler to follow |
| 2 rounds of the first-player token | Fixed 8, 10 or 14 days | Long enough to plan |
| Delivery card fruits only | The missing fruit still arrives (1) | Prevents days where nobody can sell anything |
| Event cards (6 types) | 13 events, each announced a day early | Events change decisions, not just numbers |
| First player rotates | Poorest stall buys first | Stops early leaders running away |
| (none) | Perks, upgrades, Market Days, Closing Day | Every game opens differently and has an arc |
