# Fruit Market: Rules

*Game design by Murshidi bin Md Hashim. Tabletop edition rules (rules version 18: a top-up for a stall out of cash, standing orders with a stake, today's purchases public until the bell, Favoured Trade after the flip, and new numbers; the list is in "Rules version 18" below).*

Fruit Market is a game about buying and selling fruit. Each player runs a small fruit stall that buys from the farmers and sells to the town. Fruit goes bad over time, so you have to turn it into cash fast. Every day ends with one question: **how much can I charge before my rival, or the rot, gets me?**

Every rule here can be played with cards and coins on a table; the app only does the bookkeeping.

## The big idea

Fruit Market is a game of **timing and nerve**. Three things pull against you every day:

1. **The clock.** All fruit gets older every night, and old fruit sells for less and then not at all. Whatever you buy, you are racing to sell it.
2. **The secret price.** You never see your rivals' prices until the bell. Price too high and the customer walks past you; too low and you give money away.
3. **The shut crate.** Stalls are secret. Before prices are set you learn only **how many pieces** each rival put out, not which fruit. Guess right and you can charge more; put out what they did not expect and they price for the wrong fruit.

You do not need to be the best at everything. Often the winner is simply the stall that threw away the least fruit and was brave one day when the others were not.

### One customer, start to finish

Bobby Books wants **3 blueberries** and pays up to **$5** for a fresh one (so $4 for okay; he is an Everyday customer, so he will not take soft fruit).

- Stall A has 2 **fresh** blueberries, bought at $2 each, and posts **$4**.
- Stall B has 1 **okay** blueberry, bought at $1, and posts **$3**.
- Stall C has 2 **fresh** blueberries and posts **$5**.

Bobby Books buys from the **cheapest stall** first. Stall B sells 1 at $3 (+$3, which cost $1). He still wants 2, so he moves to the next cheapest, Stall A, which sells 2 at $4 (+$8, which cost $4). His order is full, so Stall C sells nothing, even though its fruit was fresh. Because two stalls served him, nobody draws a favour card.

Had Stall A been alone in serving him, A would have drawn a favour card. That is the reason to be the one stall that has what a customer wants.

## Goal

Have the **most cash** at the end of the last day. Upgrades are worth nothing at the end. Fruit left on your shelf is worth only what the jam maker pays on Closing Day: $1 for each ripe piece, nothing for green fruit, and nothing if Closing Day is switched off. Ties go to whoever sold more fruit. If that is level too, the tied stalls share the win.

## Players and length

- 2 to 6 stalls. Each one is played by you, a friend on the same phone, or a computer player (Easy, Normal or Hard). The Market League and the Daily market stay at 2 to 4 stalls until 5 and 6 stalls have been playtested.
- **Quick** is 8 days, **Standard** is 10 days and **Long** is 14 days.
- Each stall starts with **$10**, a shelf of 8 spaces, and no favour cards.
- **Out of cash.** At dawn, from Day 2, a stall with an empty shelf and less than $5 is topped up to $5 by the market (see Morning).
- The crowd is **one customer per stall, and never more than five**: the regular plus one walk-in for every stall after the first, up to four walk-ins. With 6 stalls every customer wants one more of the first fruit on their card.

## The fruit

Every fruit uses the same freshness rule: it sells at the customer's full price when **fresh**, **$1 less when okay** and **$2 less when soft**. One rule covers every fruit's life: **the last two days are okay, then soft; every day before that is fresh.** The fruits last 3, 4, 5 and 6 days.

| Fruit | Play style | Track (oldest → freshest) | Cost: Fresh / Okay / Soft | Unripe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🫐 Blueberry | 3 days. Cheap and popular: sell it today or lose value. | soft · okay · fresh | $2 / $1 / $1 | none |
| 🍉 Watermelon | 4 days. The steady seller, with one spare fresh day. | soft · okay · fresh ×2 | $3 / $2 / $1 | none |
| 🥭 Mango | 5 days. Always arrives green for $4, ripens in 2 nights, then has one fresh day at a premium. | soft · okay · fresh | $6 / $3 / $1 | 2 nights ($4) |
| 🍎 Apple | 6 days. The keeper: four fresh days. Never the best price, never a loss. | soft · okay · fresh ×4 | $3 / $2 / $1 | none |

Every night all fruit moves one space left. Fruit that falls off the left end spoils. Unripe fruit can't be sold yet and becomes fresh as nights pass.

## Cards

- **Lorry cards (20):** twenty different lorries. Every card prints how many blueberries / watermelons / apples / mangoes it delivers with **2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 stalls**; use the column for your table. A lorry brings about 8, 11, 15, 19 or 24 pieces. Five gluts load up on one fruit (Highland Apples brings no mangoes), four pairs carry two fruits, six mixed loads carry everything (Kampung Truck brings no apples), two light loads and two heavy loads. **Every lorry delivers its mangoes green** (unripe: they ripen on the shelf in two nights; with unripe fruit switched off they arrive ripe). The **Seconds Lorry** delivers its other fruit **Okay**: it goes into each fruit's freshest Okay space at the Wholesale Market, so it costs Okay prices and Fresh-only customers will not buy it.
- **Customer cards (36):** a name and one or two fruits (about 3 pieces a card), each with an amount and the most they pay for a *fresh* one. Prices follow the type: a Bargain customer pays $1 or $2 under the usual price (blueberry $5, watermelon $7, apple $6, mango $11), a Premium customer $1 or $2 over, an Everyday customer within $1 of it. The back of each card shows the fruits they want. Each customer also has a **freshness type** (below), printed on the front. The type stays secret while a walk-in is face down.
- **Event cards (20):** one is drawn each morning from Day 2 onward. Nobody knows it in advance: it is turned over that morning. Mango Craze, Superfood Trend, Hotel Order, Kenduri and Bake-Off bring a **guest card** (below).
- **Guest cards (5):** the Influencer, the Gym Club, the Hotel Chef, the Wedding Caterer and the Bakery. A guest joins the customers at the end of the row on the day of its event, even when the row is full, and is served like any other customer.
- **Standing-order cards (8):** two for each fruit. One is turned over on each Market Day morning (see Standing orders).
- **Favour cards (12):** two of each, at every table size. A secret reward for serving a customer's whole order by yourself. Hold up to 3 (4 with the Charmer perk).

### Guest cards

| Guest | Event | Wants (fixed price for each) | Freshness type |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Gym Club | Superfood Trend | 2 blueberries at $8 | Everyday (Fresh or Okay) |
| The Influencer | Mango Craze | 1 mango at $15 | Premium (Fresh only) |
| The Hotel Chef | Hotel Order | 1 apple at $8, 1 watermelon at $8, 1 blueberry at $7 | Everyday (Fresh or Okay) |
| The Wedding Caterer | Kenduri | 3 watermelons at $8 | Everyday (Fresh or Okay) |
| The Bakery | Bake-Off | 2 apples at $7 | Everyday (Fresh or Okay) |

A guest's price is fixed: the event's own price change is **not** added on top (on a Mango Craze the Influencer still pays at most $15 for a fresh mango), and a Premium Display does not raise it. Only Closing Day adds its $1. As for anyone, okay fruit sells for $1 less and soft fruit for $2 less.

### Decks running out

- **Customers (36):** when the deck is empty, shuffle every customer card that is not on the table (regular or walk-in) into a new deck. Bigger tables reach it sooner: a 6-stall game goes through the deck about twice.
- **Favour cards (12):** played cards go to a discard pile. When the deck is empty, shuffle the discard pile into a new deck. Cards in hands stay there. If a stall's hand is full, it draws nothing.
- **Event cards (20):** if the deck is empty, shuffle all 20 again.
- **Delivery cards (20):** shuffle at set-up. Day 1 turns the top card; each later morning brings the card shown the day before. A game never uses more than 15; if the deck is empty, shuffle all 20 again.

## The queue

Customers are drawn a day ahead. Each morning, draw tomorrow's delivery card (events are never drawn ahead), tomorrow's **regular** face up and tomorrow's **walk-ins** face down, so everyone can see them while they buy. Walk-ins show only the fruit on their card backs. On Day 1, draw the first day's regular and walk-ins during set-up, then tomorrow's on the first morning as usual. Together these make the **Tomorrow strip**; when today's crowd turns face up (the moment buying ends), the Tomorrow strip stays where it is and becomes the next day's row. Nothing is drawn for the day after the last day.

- Walk-ins per day: **stalls − 1, and never more than 4**, so the row never holds more than five customers.
- Walk-in events add or take away walk-ins for every 3 stalls (rounded up), still never past 4. A **standing order** collected that day takes the place of the last walk-in when the row is full. A **guest** joins the end of the row even when it is full: the row is then six that day.
- **Nobody waits overnight.** A customer who does not get everything leaves at the end of the day.
- **No mango customers on Day 1 or Day 2.** Every mango arrives green and needs two nights, so nobody could serve them. When the rows for Day 1 and Day 2 are drawn (the regular, the walk-ins, and any walk-ins an event adds on Day 2), a card that wants a mango goes to the bottom of the deck and another is drawn. Mango Craze cannot be Day 2's event: put it at the bottom of the deck and turn over another. (With unripe fruit switched off mangoes arrive ripe, and this rule is not used.)

| Stalls | Regular + walk-ins | Festival | Tourist Bus | Monsoon Downpour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 + 1 | +1 | +2 | −1 |
| 3 | 1 + 2 | +1 | +2 | −1 |
| 4 | 1 + 3 | +1 (row full) | +1 (row full) | −2 |
| 5 | 1 + 4 | row full | row full | −2 |
| 6 | 1 + 4, each wanting 1 more | row full | row full | −2 |

(Never fewer than no walk-ins.)

## A day in five beats

### 1. Morning
1. Apply today's **event**.
2. **Out of cash.** From Day 2: a stall with an empty shelf (no pieces at all, green ones included) and less than $5 is topped up to $5 by the market. This comes after the event and any Health Inspector charge, and before the buying order is set and anyone bids or buys. It is public.
3. The farmer's lorry unloads today's card at the freshest position (mangoes at the unripe position). The lorry comes every day, Market Days included. A Mango Windfall's extra mangoes arrive ripe, in the Fresh space.
4. Draw tomorrow's delivery card, tomorrow's regular (face up) and tomorrow's walk-ins (face down) for the Tomorrow strip. Turn over today's event in step 1, never earlier. If it adds walk-ins (Festival, Tourist Bus), draw them now, face down, at the end of today's row (never past four walk-ins); if it takes walk-ins away (Monsoon Downpour), the last ones in today's row go back under the customer deck. Tomorrow's row is changed only by tomorrow's event, on tomorrow's morning.
5. On a **Market Day**, turn over a **standing order** and bid for it (see Standing orders) before anyone buys.

### 2. Buying
Each stall buys **up to 4 pieces of fruit** (5 with a Handcart), paying the cost for that fruit's freshness. Your shelf holds 8 (10 with a Handcart). **The poorest stall buys first, then the others follow clockwise from it** (a tie for poorest goes to the first of them clockwise from the first-player token; Day 1 starts at the token). A Priority favour jumps the queue. What a stall buys today is public until the bell; its older fruit stays secret (see Secret stalls).

### 3. Stocking
Today's customers arrive: a standing order collected today (at the front), the regular and the walk-ins, who turn face up now. Each stall secretly fills its **for-sale crate** with the exact pieces of ripe fruit it wants to sell: move those tokens from your shelf into the crate on your stall sheet, any freshness you like. When every stall is ready, each says **how many pieces** are in its crate, and nothing else: which fruit, and how fresh, stays behind the shutter. Only the pieces in your crate can sell today; fruit you keep back stays on your shelf (and keeps ageing). Green fruit can't go in a crate. A stall with no ripe fruit on its shelf has nothing to put out: it takes no stocking turn and no pricing turn.

### 4. The bell
Each stall secretly sets **one price per fruit** it put out, knowing only how many pieces every other stall put out. Then everyone reveals together: **prices and crates open at the bell**.

- **Price cap:** a customer pays at most their card price for a fresh fruit, $1 less for okay and $2 less for soft. When you sell, the **oldest piece in your crate that is fresh enough for your price** goes first. Only pieces in your crate can sell.
- **Freshness type:** a *Premium* customer buys **Fresh fruit only**; an *Everyday* customer buys **Fresh or Okay**; a *Bargain* customer buys **Fresh, Okay or Soft**. A stall cannot sell fruit a customer will not take, whatever the price. Premium: Rara Jenab, Ravi Rocket, Captain Ahmad, Anna the Artist, Devi Bloom, Miss Cha-Cha (and the Influencer). Bargain: Magic Kumar, Professor Pebble, Postman Pip, Coral Mei, Rockstar Grace, Inspector Wei. Everyone else is Everyday (the Gym Club, Hotel Chef, Wedding Caterer and Bakery too).
- There is **no lower limit**. On a Price Controls day there is an **upper** limit for each fruit.
- **Favoured Trade.** After the flip, a stall that holds Favoured Trade and has a price tied with another stall's price for the same fruit (both have that fruit out) may play the card. Such stalls decide one at a time, in today's buying order, in the open: play it or keep it. The card cannot be played before the flip. If no stall is eligible, the rush starts at once.

### 5. The rush
Customers buy in row order; a standing order collected today goes first (see Standing orders). For each fruit a customer wants, they buy from the **cheapest stall** that has fruit fresh enough for its price; leftover demand **spills to the next stall**.

- **Ties:** Favoured Trade (played after the flip; if two tied stalls both played it, the rest of this order decides between them), then fresher fruit (compare the age of the freshest piece each stall would hand over to this customer: the younger piece wins, so a piece of age 0 beats a piece of age 2 even though both are fresh), then the Haggler, then whoever bought earlier today. (Today's buying order was set at dawn: the poorest stall, then clockwise. Cash is not used, because it changes while customers are paid.)
- You're paid as soon as you sell.
- **Favours:** serve a customer's entire remaining order by yourself and draw a favour card (at most one per day; two for the Charmer).
- **Pasar Malam:** on that event's day, after the last customer, the night hawker buys 1 of each fruit still left in each stall's crate for $3, oldest piece first, any freshness.
- A customer who doesn't get everything leaves at the end of the day. Nobody waits overnight.

### Evening and night
- **Evening:** play evening favours (Priority, Gossip). Only one Priority card can be played each evening: a second one is refused and stays in its owner's hand. On the evening after each Market Day (days 3, 6, 9 and 12), build **one stall upgrade** from those you have not built (one only, that evening; a stall has room for two). Pak Din (Shade Cloth) names tonight's fruit. Farah (Sharp Ears) peeks at one of tomorrow's walk-ins.
- **Who takes an evening turn:** only a stall with something to decide: an upgrade on offer that it can afford and has room for, an evening favour card it can play, or the Shade Cloth's fruit to name. If no stall has, there is no evening and night falls.
- **Night:** all fruit at the market and in every stall ages one step (none after a Monsoon Downpour). Spoiled fruit is thrown away.

## Market rhythm

- **Market Days (days 3, 6, 9 and 12, never the last day):** a standing order is turned over in the morning (when its due day still falls inside the game), and each stall may build an upgrade that evening. The lorry still comes. A Market Day no longer adds walk-ins.
- **Closing Day (final day):** customers pay $1 more. Afterwards the **jam maker** buys every stall's leftover ripe fruit for $1 each.

Market Days and Closing Day are on by default and can be switched off in the set-up.

## Standing orders

A standing order is one big public order, turned over on a Market Day morning and collected **two days later**. It wants one fruit: **3 pieces at 2 or 3 stalls, 4 at 4 stalls, 5 at 5 or 6**, Fresh or Okay.

1. **Bid.** Before anyone buys, every stall decides whether to bid: the price per piece it would fill the order for, from $1 up to the card's ceiling, or a pass. **Who is bidding and who has passed is public; the prices are secret.** A stall may change its bid, or pass after all, for as long as any stall is still deciding. When the last stall decides, all bids are shown together. **The lowest bid wins** and holds the order. Ties go to a stall with an Order Book, then to the stall earlier in today's buying order. If everyone passes, the order is discarded.
2. **The stake.** A stall may only bid if it has the card's stake in cash (half, rounded down, with an Order Book). It may always pass. The winner pays the stake the moment it wins: the money is held on the card.
3. **Get ready.** The holder has three buying turns (today, tomorrow and the due day) to have the pieces on its shelf.
4. **Collect.** On the due day the order stands at the front of the row and is served first, from the **holder's crate only**. It takes the holder's Fresh or Okay pieces of that fruit, freshest first, up to the number it wants, and pays the holder its bid for each ($2 more for each with an Order Book). Rivals' fruit is never taken and rivals are paid nothing. Any part the order did not get simply leaves.
5. **The stake comes back, or not.** If the holder delivered every piece, it gets the stake back at once. If not, the stake is lost. There is no other fine.

| Order | Fruit | Ceiling (the highest bid) | Stake | Stake with an Order Book |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The School Canteen, The Smoothie Stand | Blueberry | $9 | $6 | $3 |
| The Beach Resort, The Company Picnic | Watermelon | $12 | $8 | $4 |
| The Pie Factory, The Cider Press | Apple | $11 | $7 | $3 |
| The Airline Caterer, The Royal Banquet | Mango | $18 | $12 | $6 |

A standing order draws no favour card and pays no tips (a Juice Bar adds nothing to its pieces either), and no event or Closing Day changes what it pays. No standing order is turned over if it could not be collected by the last day. Standing orders are not used in the Basic game.

## Shopkeepers

Before the game, each stall picks one of eight shopkeepers, so even the sixth stall chooses from at least three. A shopkeeper gives one perk for the whole game, and everyone can see it. **No two stalls can be the same shopkeeper.** Computer stalls take the shopkeepers the people did not pick. On a table, lay the eight shopkeeper cards face up and pick in turn, starting with the player to the left of the first-player token. In the app, pick on the set-up screen, or when you enter an online room (a taken shopkeeper is greyed out, and you can change yours in the lobby until the game starts).

## Playing on a table

The rules above are the same on a table and in the app. These are the ways to run them by hand (added in rules version 5; none of them changes a rule, apart from the tie-break line above).

**Order of customers.** Lay the customer cards in one row, always in this order: a standing order collected today, the regular, the walk-ins in the order they were drawn, then a special guest such as the Hotel Chef. Serve them left to right.

**The crier.** One player reads the customers aloud and pays the stalls from the bank, so stalls never swap coins with each other. Use the player who bought last that morning. For each customer line the crier says the real top price for the day (card price plus any event, Closing Day or Fixed prices change), then which fruit qualifies at each price: at **top − 2 or less any fruit** qualifies (soft included), at **top − 1 fresh or okay**, at **the top itself fresh only**, and above the top nothing (a Premium Display may go $1 over for fresh fruit, but never for a guest). The cheapest posted price sells first, and each stall hands over its oldest piece that still qualifies. On a tie, the stall whose freshest handed-over piece is younger sells first.

**Money.** The printed game keeps cash on a cash track on each stall's counter card, in front of the shutter, so everyone can read everyone's cash (cash is public). Without the printed track, use paper notes, not coins. In computer games a stall finishes a 10-day game with about $90 on average: the winner with about $110 to $130, the last stall with about $50 to $80. A bank of 60 × $1, 30 × $5, 20 × $10 and 10 × $50 is enough. The crier is the bank (see above), so stalls never swap notes with each other.

**Components for 6 stalls.** About 50 tokens of each fruit, 6 of each upgrade, 8 standing-order cards and 12 favour cards.

**Shade Cloth.** At night, Pak Din lays a fruit token on his stall sheet to name the fruit that does not age; take it back at dawn.

**Sharp Ears.** In the evening, Farah looks at the front of one of tomorrow's walk-ins, like Scout. Mark it with her colour. When she sells that customer anything, the crier pays her tip. She is tipped at most once a day.

**The stall sheet, the shutter and the counter.** Each stall has one printed sheet with a shelf row and a crate row for each fruit, a shutter that stands in front of it all game so rivals cannot see, and a counter card that lies in front of the shutter. Move the fruit you will sell from the shelf row up to the crate row, say how many pieces that is, and write your price tags, all behind the shutter. At the bell flip your tags and move your crate pieces onto your counter, each into the box with the same letter and place. Unsold counter fruit goes back to the same box of the shelf row at the end of the day. The shelf is never shown.

**Order circles.** Every customer card has one printed circle for each unit it wants, grouped by fruit. A fruit piece that is sold sits on a circle. Empty circles are what the customer still wants, so a waiting customer simply keeps its pieces on the card.

**Scout.** Turn one face-down walk-in card face up for yourself only, look, and turn it back. Say out loud that you played Scout.

**The sole-supplier cube.** The first stall to sell to a customer puts a cube of its colour on the card. If a second stall sells to the same customer, the cube comes off. When the order is complete, a cube left on the card means that stall served it alone: it draws a favour card (and a Tip Jar takes its $2 tip). If the order is complete and the cube came off, a Tip Jar stall that sold that customer anything takes $1.

**First-player token.** On Day 1 give it to a random stall. It moves one seat clockwise every night.

**Dawn line-up.** At dawn every stall counts its cash. The stall with the fewest coins goes first, then the others follow clockwise round the table from it; a tie for fewest goes to the first of them clockwise from the first-player token (on Day 1 the order is simply the seats from the token). Today's buying order, the evening order and the last tie-break all use this row. A Priority card moves its owner to the front. On Day 3 Nest Egg goes first, or second behind a Priority card. A stall that is topped up (empty shelf, less than $5) takes its top-up before the cash is counted.

**Evening.** Keep favour cards face down in front of you, so everyone can count them. A stall takes an evening turn, in line-up order, only if it has something to decide: an upgrade it can afford and has room for after a Market Day, an evening favour card it can play, or the Shade Cloth's fruit to name.

**Favoured Trade.** When the tags are flipped, look for tied prices. A stall with a tied price that holds Favoured Trade may play it now. Tied holders decide one at a time, in today's buying order, in the open.

**Today's purchases.** What you bought today is public until the bell: tell anyone who asks which pieces you took today. After the bell you need not say.

**Two ways to track age.** On the printed mat, tokens slide one slot per night and each slot shows how fresh the fruit is. With day chips, the piles keep still. Both give the same ages. The rest of this section describes chips, which also work on a stall's own shelf.

**Day chips instead of moving fruit.** Fruit piles keep still. A *fruit clock* marker shows the clock number: it starts at 1 and moves on 1 each night (2 after a Heatwave night). Every pile of fruit carries a chip with the clock number on the day it became fresh. A pile's **age** is the clock number minus its chip. A fruit bought **unripe** (only mangoes arrive green) gets the chip of the clock number on which it ripens (the clock number now plus 2); it is green until the clock reaches that number.

| Age | Blueberry | Watermelon | Apple | Mango |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | fresh | fresh | fresh | fresh |
| 1 | okay | fresh | fresh | okay |
| 2 | soft | okay | fresh | soft |
| 3 | spoiled | soft | fresh | spoiled |
| 4 | | spoiled | okay | |
| 5 | | | soft | |
| 6 | | | spoiled | |

At dawn, throw away piles that have reached "spoiled". A Health Inspector, if one is in town, counts the "soft" piles at the start of the day, before anyone buys or throws fruit away. A Juice Bar juices up to 2 pieces of fruit that spoil. After a Monsoon Downpour no clock moves. A Shade Cloth pile keeps its chip for the night (add 1 to the chips of the named fruit). A **Fridge** adds 1 to the chips of that stall's ripe piles, never past today's clock number.

**No waiting (rules 14).** A customer who is not fully served leaves at the end of the day; their pieces go back to the supply.

*Left out on purpose: a price ladder board for lining up posted prices. Sorting the price cards by hand works for 2 to 6 stalls.*

## Secret stalls (rules version 15)

- **Your stall is secret.** Nobody else sees your shelf, at any time. (In the app a rival's box shows cash only.)
- **Buying is in the open.** Everyone can watch what you take from the Wholesale Market. Rules 18: what you bought today stays public until the bell (in the app, small fruit marks on your box); what you bought on earlier days is for rivals to remember if they can.
- **After stocking, one number.** When every stall is ready, each says how many pieces are in its crate. Not which fruit, not how fresh.
- **At the bell, crates open.** Prices and crates are revealed together: which fruit each stall is selling, how many, and at what price. Shelves stay hidden.
- **Computer stalls play by the same rule.** They price from the piece counts, today's orders and what they saw each stall buy; they never read a crate before the bell, so they can be fooled. Rules 18: an Easy computer uses none of what it saw bought, a Normal computer only today's purchases, a Hard computer everything it saw.

## Rules version 18

What changed from rules version 17, in plain words.

- **Out of cash.** At dawn, from Day 2, a stall with an empty shelf and less than $5 is topped up to $5 by the market.
- **Standing orders.** The winner puts down a stake (the number that used to be the fine) when it wins. The order is filled from the winner's crate only. A full delivery brings the stake back; anything less loses it. There are no fines, rivals' fruit is never taken, and rivals are paid nothing.
- **Order Book.** Costs $3. It wins tied bids, halves the stake, and adds $2 to what an order pays for each piece.
- **Ripening Room.** Costs $4. Its owner's green mangoes ripen in one night. (A $1 discount on green mangoes was built and then dropped before release: it undid the dearer green mango.)
- **Juice Bar** costs $6 and **Premium Display** $5. The Handcart stays at $6.
- **Tip Jar.** $1 from a customer who leaves with their whole order if Kiki sold them any of it, $2 if she served them alone.
- **Nest Egg** starts with $15.
- **Charmer.** Ah Chen starts with 3 favour cards (4 before). She still holds up to 4 and earns up to 2 a day.
- **Sharp Ears.** The tip is $2 at 3 to 6 stalls and $1 at 2 stalls, at most once a day. Farah peeks in the evening.
- **Green mango.** $4 at the market.
- **Customers.** Coral Mei, Magic Kumar and Professor Pebble pay $4 for a blueberry.
- **Lorries.** Short Load brings 2/2/1/1 at 3 stalls. Summer Run brings 2 apples at 5 and 6 stalls.
- **Mango Windfall.** The extra mangoes arrive ripe.
- **Blueberry Glut.** Blueberries also cost $1 less at the market that day.
- **Days 1 and 2.** No customer wants a mango, and Mango Craze is never Day 2's event.
- **Today's purchases** are public until the bell. Older fruit stays secret.
- **Favoured Trade** is played after prices flip, in the open, by a stall with a tied price.
- **Juice Bar's $1** is for sales to customers and guests in the rush only.
- **Premium Display** never raises a guest's fixed price.
- **Priority.** One card an evening. On Day 3 it goes ahead of Nest Egg.
- **The end.** A tie on cash goes to the stall that sold more fruit. If that is level too, the stalls share the win.
- **Favour deck.** 12 cards at every table size.
- **Guests** join the row even when it is full.
- **Nothing to decide.** A stall with no ripe fruit takes no stocking or pricing turn. A stall takes an evening turn only if it has a choice.
- **In the app** computer players changed too: a Normal computer no longer prices as if it held the Haggler's tie-break, and computers choose upgrades by what each is worth.

## Open bidding and upgrades (rules version 17)

- **Bidding in the open.** For a standing order everyone sees who is bidding, who passed and who is still deciding. Prices stay secret until the last stall decides, and until then anyone may change their bid or pass.
- (Rules 17 also paid a rival who filled an order's gap $1 under the holder's bid. Rules 18 took rivals out of standing orders.)
- **All five upgrades.** After a Market Day every upgrade a stall has not built is on offer, as on the table.
- **In the app** computer stalls decide first, so you see them before you bid.

## Tomorrow's queue while buying (rules version 16)

- **Tomorrow's crowd comes out in the morning.** With tomorrow's lorry card, draw tomorrow's regular face up and tomorrow's walk-ins face down. Everyone buys knowing today's crowd and tomorrow's.
- **Nothing else moves.** Today's walk-ins still turn face up when buying ends; tomorrow's event still changes tomorrow's walk-ins on tomorrow's morning; Gossip, Scout and Sharp Ears look at the same cards as before.
- **In the app** the Tomorrow strip under today's tickets now shows during buying too.

## Daily market

Each day has a small twist, the same for everyone, rotating through seven: the usual game, a quick 8-day game, no upgrades, fixed prices, no events, a long 14-day game, and the basic game.

Everyone in the world plays the same shuffled decks each day: you against Rosa (Normal), Uncle Benny (Hard) and Ah Chen (Normal) over 10 days. Scores are kept per rules version, and per version of the app's computer players (scores are checked by replaying the game with them).

## What changed from the paper draft

| Paper draft | This edition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Players take turns undercutting in the open | Crates shown first, then one secret price per fruit revealed at the bell | One clear dramatic moment a day |
| Paid when a customer card leaves the market | Paid immediately on each sale | Simpler to follow |
| 2 rounds of the first-player token | Fixed 8, 10 or 14 days | Long enough to plan |
| Delivery card fruits only | The missing fruit still arrives (1) | Prevents days where nobody can sell anything |
| Event cards (6 types) | 20 events, each a surprise on the morning it is drawn | Every morning can change the day |
| First player rotates | Poorest stall buys first | Stops early leaders running away |
| (none) | Perks, upgrades, Market Days, Closing Day | Every game opens differently and has an arc |
